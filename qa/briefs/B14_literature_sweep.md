# Builder brief B14: web sweep for carrying-capacity and food-capacity studies, 2011 to 2026

Rules: write only `qa/reports/B14_literature_sweep.csv` and `qa/reports/B14_literature_sweep.md`; read anything; no subagents; no git; plain language; no time words. You may use web search and web fetch. Never download a PDF into either repository. Every row must rest on a page you opened; record its URL. Mark every row `not read` (you record existence and abstract-level content only).

Goal: a candidate list of published studies from 2011 to 2026 that a methods review of carrying-capacity estimation (for land use planners, Australia as the case) would be expected to cite, so the coordinator and Murray can see what the project's literature register (`qa/literature_register.csv`, read it first so you do not repeat its rows) lacks.

Search in this order, recording what you searched: (1) Australian studies: population carrying capacity, food capacity, food security and land-use futures for Australia (CSIRO Australian National Outlook 2015 and 2019 and the Hatfield-Dodds paper; Bryan et al. 2016 land-use trajectories; Sobels et al. 2010 for the immigration department; ABARES food security 2023; Ridoutt and Hendrie CSIRO diet-footprint studies; State of the Environment 2021 land chapter; Sustainable Population Australia and Australian Academy of Science statements; any paper citing Lane, Dawes and Grace 2014 or 2015 or Lane 2017); (2) resource-based and land-based capacity studies elsewhere (Peters 2016 family, Zumkehr and Campbell 2015, local foodshed studies, Fairlie-type national studies); (3) land per diet and nutrition-optimisation studies (EAT-Lancet 2019, Springmann 2018, Gerten 2020, Poore and Nemecek 2018, Roos 2017, Kastner 2012, van Zanten 2016, Erb 2016, Muller 2017, Clark and Tilman 2017); (4) footprint methods and critiques since 2011 (Borucke 2013, Giampietro and Saltelli 2014, Blomqvist 2013, Lin 2018, the Global Footprint Network); (5) reviews of the carrying-capacity concept since 2011 (Chapman and Byron 2018 and others). For each candidate: authors, year, title, journal or publisher, DOI or URL (verified by opening the page), one line on what it estimates or argues (from the abstract or the page), the theme number, whether it is Australian, and whether it is already in the project's literature register.

CSV columns: `theme, australian, authors, year, title, venue, doi_or_url, what_it_does, in_register, status`. The md: what you searched, how many candidates per theme, which searches returned nothing, and the ten you judge most important for the paper with one line each on why.
