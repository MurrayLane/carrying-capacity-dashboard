# Analysis brief R05b_response_plan_B: a reasoned response plan to the referee reports on Paper B (methods)

You are the author's analyst, not an advocate and not a referee. Your job is to test every major issue in `qa/reports/R04c_rereview_B_ecological_economist.md` and `qa/reports/R04d_rereview_B_modeller.md` against the project's records and say what is true, what the referee has missed or misread, what the fair response is, and what it would cost and change. Rules: plain language; no em dashes; no time words; decision 33 in tone. No git; no subagents; no network. Write only `qa/reports/R05b_response_plan_B.md`. Change nothing else.

Read, in this order: the two referee reports; `papers/B_methods/draft/paper_B_draft1.md` and its supplement; the registers (`qa/claims_register.csv`, `qa/literature_register.csv`, `qa/sources_register.csv`, `qa/defects.csv`); `qa/DECISIONS.md`; the analysis READMEs the issues touch (`analysis/method_tests_2025/README.md` and its csvs (`harmonised_comparison.py` and csv, `gap_attribution.py` and csv, `cross_method.py`), `analysis/comparators_2025/README.md`, `analysis/sensitivity_2025/README.md`, `analysis/nutrition_lp_2025/README.md`, `analysis/resilience_2025/README.md`, `papers/B_methods/SECTION7_DRAFT.md`, `papers/B_methods/SECTION_SCOPE.md`); `ERRATA.md`; `qa/reports/PROVENANCE_AUDIT_2026-11-13.md`.

For every major issue in both reports (merge the two referees' issues where they overlap; keep each referee's wording in a quote), write a block:
1. **The issue** in one sentence, with the referee and number.
2. **What the records show.** Check the factual premise against the files: is the referee right, partly right, or mistaken, and on what evidence (file, cell, row, page). Where a referee asserts something about the data or the method that the records contradict, say so and cite the record. Where the referee has found something the audit missed, say that too.
3. **Does it change a finding?** Which headline claims (name the claim ids and the sentences in the abstract or highlights) would move if the referee is right, and by roughly how much, from what the records already hold (for example the local-productivity footprint readings, the herd-cycle range, the three yield bases, the alternative decomposition orders if any run exists).
4. **The fair response**, one of: compute (name the analysis, its inputs, which script would be extended, its rough cost on the calibrated basis in the HANDOFF note); reword (what the sentence should say instead); rebut (the evidence, in the words a response letter would use, without conceding what is true); out of scope (why, and what the paper should say so a reader does not expect it). Where two responses are defensible, give both with a recommendation and the reason, and say what a reviewer would likely accept.
5. **Dependencies and conflicts**: whether this issue's resolution depends on another's, and whether the two referees disagree on it.

Then: (a) a table of all issues (number; referee; verdict on the premise; response type; headline effect; cost); (b) the three to five changes that would do most to satisfy both referees, in order, each in one sentence; (c) the places where accepting the criticism would be wrong and why, each in one sentence; (d) the questions to the author from both reports, each with a one-line answer from the records or "needs Murray"; (e) the minor issues grouped by kind with the fix for each kind; (f) the register rows touched (none) and the words "Wrong-value check:" with one deliberate example of a response that would concede something the records show to be false, marked as an example.

**Second round.** These are the second-round reports on the revised drafts. Also read the first-round plan (`qa/reports/R03a_response_plan_A.md` or `R03b_response_plan_B.md`) and `qa/DECISIONS.md` rows 100 to 132, and for every second-round issue say whether it is (i) new, (ii) a first-round issue the revision answered and the referee did not accept (say what was done and whether it was enough), or (iii) a first-round issue the revision did not reach. Weigh in particular: what was computed since the first round (B110 to B130 and their checks, in `qa/reports/`), so that an issue already answered by a file is marked "reword to cite the file" rather than "compute". Where a referee asks for something that a standing decision rules out (decisions 33, 113, 118, 122), say so and give the response-letter wording. End with (g) a decision table for the author: option; recommendation; confidence; cost as a share of the weekly allowance on the HANDOFF calibration; reason; and (h) one paragraph on whether a third round would be needed after these changes.
