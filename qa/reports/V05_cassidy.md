# V05 verification report: people nourished per hectare (Cassidy et al. 2013), independent rebuild (Opus checker, blind), 2026-11-07

**Against the project's test (`cassidy_people_per_hectare.py`).** Australia 2021-23: checker 9.96 produced and 5.77 delivered people a hectare on 25.1 Mha (about 50 crops); project 10.5 and 6.0 on 24.5 Mha (21 crops): 5% and 4% apart. Australia 2011: checker 7.55 and 4.26; project 7.2 and 4.1 (5%, 4%). World 2021-23: checker 12.8 and 7.3; project 13.7 and 7.9 (7%, 8%). Calorie shares agree within two points (Australia 52/41/8 against 51/42/7). People fed by Australia's delivered calories: checker about 145 million (5.77 x 25.1); project 147. The paper's about-2000 world values (10.1 and 6.0) are reproduced in both builds at 1.14 to 1.2 times, as expected from yield growth.

**Where the builds differ, and what moves the answer.** Crop set (50 against 21 items), calorie values (standard values chosen by each), the allocation of exported crops (the checker shows this choice alone moves Australia's delivered figure by up to 0.8 people a hectare, the largest single effect), the feed-efficiency weighting (the paper implies 10% to 12%; the checker's weighting gives 12% for Australia and 16% for the world), and a 2.5 Mt disagreement between FAO's two production series for 2011 Australian wheat (season timing).

**Verdict.** Holds within 5% for Australia and 8% for the world; the method's own stated-choice sensitivity (the export allocation) is larger than the disagreement between the builds, so the Cassidy row should carry a range (Australia 5.8 to 6.0 delivered; about 145 to 147 million) rather than a point. Defect X043 (range, not a fault).

**Finding worth the paper.** Both builds show Australia feeds fewer people per hectare of cropland than the world average on both measures, despite large exports: low calorie yields per hectare (7.4 against 11.3 million kcal in 2011) and about 41% to 43% of calories going to feed, much of it exported canola meal and feed grains.
