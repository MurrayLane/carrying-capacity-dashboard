# B45: Wackernagel's 1994 UBC thesis, the food land factors

**Source read.** `Literature review/Wackernagel_1994_PhD_thesis_UBC_Ecological_footprint_and_appropriated_carrying_capacity.pdf` (357 scanned pages). Pages read as images with the Read tool, ten or fewer at a time: PDF 1 to 12 (contents; the first image batch was dropped by the tool and the contents were then taken from the scan's embedded text layer), 80, 117 to 146, 229 to 238, 248 to 275, 289 to 295, 300 to 308, 314, 317 to 341. The rest was skimmed through the embedded text layer for headings and for food, crop, pasture, meat, wool, Australia and handbook. Context: B43, B44, V07, `simpson_food.py` (the 1991-92 consumption quantities).

**Short answer.** The thesis prints what the 1996 book only names. The pasture productivity is a stocking rate from Ensminger 1978, *The Stockman's Handbook* (pp. 593 to 637): **1.24 cattle (animal units) a hectare**. From it the thesis derives **0.00322 ha yr per kg of meat** (all Canadian meat, about 311 kg/ha) for cattle pasture, and **34 kg of sheep meat a hectare a year** and 10.5 kg of wool (14.2 kg net) for sheep. Crop yields are the FAO 1990 world yields in Table A2.2 (cereals 2,646 kg/ha). Applied to Simpson's 1991-92 Australian consumption, **all red meat at the thesis's sheep rate gives 1.713 ha of pasture against Simpson's 1.758 (2.6% low)**: V07's fitted 33.2 kg/ha is the thesis's 34 kg/ha of sheep meat. The cattle rate gives 0.188 ha. The plant-food crop reaches only 0.071 ha at the thesis world yields (0.094 with garden items moved to crop) against 0.312. The animal feed crop falls at 0.086 to 0.116 ha against 0.095. Wool at 14.2 kg/ha would need 12.1 kg of clean wool a person.

**Treatment of meat, dairy and wool against feed grain.** Both apply at once. Cattle stand on pasture (e12, 0.33 ha for milk and meat cattle) and the same animal products are also charged feed grain on cropland (d12, 0.42 ha). Wool is charged to the clothing row (e41). The pasture rate per kg is computed against all meat consumed, pork and poultry included (2,423,435 t, Table A2.3), though only cattle are put on pasture; sheep are not in the Canadian e12 cell at all.

## 4. Answer to question 3

Inputs: 1991-92 Australian consumption as in V07 and `simpson_food.py`: beef 37.4, lamb and mutton 21.0 (red meat 58.4), pork 19.3, poultry 25.9, eggs 12.4, milk 186.4 (23.3 kg solids at 12.5%), cereals 89.2, potatoes 66.6, other vegetables 86.6, fruit 120.1, sugar 45.6, oil 17.0, nuts 6.5, pulses 0 kg a person a year. The thesis supplies three candidate bases for pasture and one for crop.

**Pasture, Simpson 1.758 ha.**

| Basis (thesis page) | Rate | Australian 1991-92 | Gap to 1.758 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cattle pasture per kg of all meat, 0.00322 ha yr/kg (p. 284) | 311 kg meat/ha | 58.4 x 0.00322 = **0.188** | 1.570 short (11%) |
| Pasture plus feed crop, 0.00779 (129 kg/ha, p. 284) | 129 kg/ha | 0.453 | 1.305 short |
| Sheep meat, 34 kg/ha/yr (5.5 x 5 x 1.24, p. 285), applied to all red meat | 34.1 kg/ha | 58.4 / 34.1 = **1.713** | 0.045 short (2.6%) |
| Thesis method extended to cattle with its own Table A2.3 world figures: beef 49,436,000 t / 1,281,472,000 cattle = 38.6 kg a head, x 1.24 = 47.8 kg/ha; sheep 34.1 kg/ha | | beef 0.782 + sheep 0.616 = 1.398 | 0.360 short (20%) |
| As above plus milk: 474,020,000 t / 222,846,000 milk animals x 1.24 = 2,638 kg/ha | | 1.398 + 0.071 = 1.469 | 0.289 short (16%) |

The fitted 33.2 kg of red meat a hectare (V07) matches the thesis's sheep-meat rate of 34 kg/ha/yr (world sheep meat per head, 5 sheep to an animal unit, Ensminger's 1.24 animal units a hectare) within 2.6%. It is about a tenth of the thesis's Canadian cattle rate (311 kg/ha, a rate inflated by dividing cattle pasture by all meat, pork and poultry included), a quarter of its combined pasture and feed rate (129 kg/ha), and 1.67 times the FAO 1990-92 world ruminant rate (19.9 kg/ha, V07). The best reading is that Simpson charged all Australian red meat, beef included, at the sheep rate and put no dairy on pasture, which fits his text (meat and wool only) rather than his key. The thesis does not say to do that; the residual 0.045 ha could be a slightly different red-meat figure (59.8 kg at 34.1 kg/ha gives 1.758) or rounding. Not proven without his worksheet.

**Plant-food crop, Simpson 0.312 ha**, at Table A2.2 world yields: cereals 89.2 / 2,646 = 0.0337; potatoes 66.6 / 15,315 = 0.0043; sugar as cane 45.6 x 13 / 60,229 = 0.0098 (as beet, 6:1 at 35,573, 0.0077); oil 17.0 / 1,000 (the thesis's placeholder yield for oils; it prints no oil yield) = 0.0170; nuts 6.5 / 1,000 = 0.0065. **Crop 0.071 ha.** Garden, the thesis's convention for fruit and vegetables: vegetables 86.6 / 15,000 + fruit 120.1 / 7,000 = 0.023 (0.014 at the flat 15 t/ha of c11), against Simpson's garden 0.032. Even with fruit and vegetables put in crop the total is 0.094. **Gap 0.241 ha (the thesis gives 23% of 0.312).** To reach 0.312 with the other items fixed, cereals would need 726 kg a person at 2,646 kg/ha, the scale of total grain use including feed (V07). The thesis's alternative crop routes are national-area routes (d10 2nd calculation: cropland per person times the domestic share; d11 2nd calculation), which for Canada give 0.92 and 0.21; an Australian equivalent would need Australia's domestic grain share, not on hand.

**Animal-product feed crop, Simpson 0.095 ha.** The thesis's 5th calculation (energy times feed ratio / 13,020 kJ/kg / 2,646 kg/ha):
- All animal products at 8:1 with Table A2.3 energies (meat 13,000, eggs 6,120, milk 2,720 kJ/kg): 1.93E9 J a person, 0.448 ha (4.7 times Simpson; it double-counts the ruminants already on pasture, as the Canadian matrix does).
- Pork, poultry and eggs only at the thesis's 6:1: 0.116 ha with Table A2.3 energies; 0.086 ha with Table A2.4 energies (pork 11,000, poultry 8,000, eggs 6,000).
So 0.095 sits inside the thesis's range for non-ruminants (0.086 to 0.116), consistent with V07 that this cell reproduces.

**Wool pasture, Simpson 0.854 ha.** The thesis rate is 14.2 kg/ha net (10.5 gross). 0.854 ha implies 12.1 kg of clean wool a person a year (9.0 kg at the gross rate), 6.7 times the thesis's assumed OECD consumption of 1.8 kg (which gives 0.13 ha). The thesis cannot reproduce 0.854 without an Australian wool figure; it suggests Simpson used a much larger wool quantity, possibly production rather than consumption.

## 5. What the thesis does not contain

- No Australian figure of any kind, and no regional analysis of Australia.
- No beef-specific pasture rate: the cattle rate is per kg of all meat consumed; the only ruminant-specific meat rate is for sheep (34 kg/ha). No dairy rate per kg of milk (dairy enters only as milk cattle head counts on pasture).
- No vegetable-oil yield (only seed yields and 1000 kg/ha placeholders) and no sugar yield as sugar (only cane and beet, with conversion notes).
- No Vancouver or Lower Fraser Valley food calculation beyond scaling the Canadian average.
- No rule for sharing pasture among beef, sheep and milk, and no statement that sheep pasture enters the food row (in Canada sheep appear only under wool).
- The 1993 handbook is cited but not reproduced; only the 1993 brochure is. Ensminger's stocking rate is quoted as a single figure; the handbook pages behind it (593 to 637) are not reproduced.
