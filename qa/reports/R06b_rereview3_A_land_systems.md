# Referee report, third round: Paper A (results), land-systems referee

Submitted to Population and Environment. I read my second-round report, the response letter (round 2), the revised paper (main text only; I have not read the appended drafter notes or treated the bracketed flags as content), the supplement and the highlights, concentrating on sections 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8.1, 9.1 and 12 and Supplementary Sections S1, S4, S8, S10 and S12.

## 1. Recommendation

**Minor revision.** The headline claims now carry their uncertainty: the paired test, the two drought bounds, the ranged failure probability and the merged land and livestock layer answer most of what I asked. What remains is framing and consistency that can be fixed in text without new model runs.

## 2. Major points of the second round

**1. Climate phase of the input windows. Partly answered.** The letter is accurate: Table 1 gives every window, but "the paper does not label the climate phase of each window." The evidence offered does not reach the point. All ten three-year subsets in Table S16 come from 2005-06 to 2009-10, so every one is a Millennium Drought subset. Their narrow spread (32.7 to 34.0 million) shows the result is stable inside that window, not that the 2011 window is typical. The herd-cycle range for 2025 (39.4 to 43.8 million) is likewise compared with a drought-window 2011 base. I accept the reason for not applying a cereal normalisation to meat, since I asked for that factor to be treated as a bound. I do not accept leaving the phase unlabelled: it costs a sentence, and the paired test of section 9.1 covers only shared settings, not the windows.

**2. ABS frame and land layer. Answered in substance, one request declined acceptably.** Merging land and livestock into one layer is the right fix, and the Avon and national ratios in S1 (1.097 against 1.104) show the cancellation clearly. The 0.19 million upper bound in section 4 and S10 matches the letter. The 0.4 and 2.5 bound test is done. I accept not rerunning on the ABARES land-use maps given the cancellation argument and that the land source is already a sampled choice. The text says the post-2008-09 threshold and frame changes "are not documented in the sources read here." That is honest, but the ABS explanatory notes to the agricultural census are a quick read and would let the paper name the change rather than its absence.

**3. Capacity as a production-to-consumption ratio. Answered.** Section 6 now says what the letter says, and calls it a consistency check, not a validation, with the reasons (all meats, ratios not counts). One residue: S10 shows red meat (2.5 and 3.0) and milk (2.0 and 1.5) do not match the model, so "Short-term capacity therefore reads as a statement of how far meat production covers meat eaten" should be softened to "is close to".

**4. Grain-fed and pasture-fed livestock. Partly answered.** The yield at which cropland carries pasture-fed output is now stated (section 6), which answers my question 2. On feedlots, section 6 says "the land that grows the feedlot grain and the feedlot's own land are in its denominator", but Table S11 and S1 describe the livestock denominator as "the 2020-21 grazing area of the land layer" (plus hay in S1). These need to agree. The rise in feedlot share between the two windows is not quantified, and the claim about pooled land need is argued, not computed, as the paper says. The test letting arable grazing land in the mixed zone carry crops is not run and the letter gives no reason; I can accept that for this paper only if section 12 says the rule is untested where grazing land is arable, since the 37% and the abstract's "partition effect" rest on it.

**5. Cereal factor on pasture-fed output. Answered.** Both bounds are in the abstract, highlight 3, section 8.1 and conclusion 3; the record-period and area-weighted variants are in Table S13; the failure probability is a range and is out of the conclusions. The paper says what the letter says. See section 3 below for one new sentence.

**6. Paired 2011 to 2025 change. Answered.** Section 9.1 matches the letter, including the holdings-only result and the long-term change not being established. Table 7 is on one weather table and S14 holds the thesis-table rows; the finding that the apparent 1-in-20 improvement was the table is stated plainly.

**7. NRM boundaries. Answered acceptably.** Section 2 states the fixed-partition wording. The letter's "SA4 results are in Table 4" is true only of the scale-curve sums, but the planning claim in 1.3 is modest enough to stand.

**8. Diet layer. Answered.** "Availability, not intake", the absence of a second series and the ten-year window (39.1 million; 2025 still 17% to 19% above 2011) are all in section 4 and S10, matching the letter.

**9. Literature. Not answered.** The reason is word count. I accept it for the works not held. It is weaker for Peters and colleagues (2016) and Bryan and colleagues (2016), which the letter says were read: Peters is the closest published treatment of the cultivated against grazing-only constraint that section 6 calls the mechanism of the whole paper.

**10. Long-term preset. Answered.** The abstract defines the preset and names weather and fuel; section 7.1 and conclusion 2 give 79% at a 1-in-20 factor. Table S10 confirms the linearity the 79% relies on.

## 3. New problems introduced by the revision

1. Section 8.1: "Neither bound is preferred; a drought's effect on pasture-fed output lies between them." The paper's own single-year evidence suggests same-year pasture-fed output can stay level or rise with destocking and fall a year later, so the effect is not shown to lie between the bounds in the drought year itself. Suggest "over a drought and the year after it, the effect on pasture-fed output is expected to lie between them."
2. Supplementary Section S4, the single-year check: "the drought years 2018-19 and 2019-20 give 48.8 and 48.3 million, near the median ... as the herd rebuilt", followed by "2016-17 to 2019-20 stay on placeholders" for the livestock series. If the drought years' livestock series are placeholders, the near-median result says nothing about destocking, and the letter's use of it in reply to my point 5 overstates it. Say plainly which years carry observed livestock data.
3. Section 6 against Table S11 and S1 on what the livestock-yield denominator contains (point 4 above).
4. Table 1 gives 2025 field-crop yields as 2022-23 to 2024-25, while Table S11 lists NRM cubes for 2016-17 to 2020-21 as well, and S4 samples a crop-yield trend window between the two. One sentence under Table 1 should say which is the base.

## 5. Three changes I would still want before acceptance

1. Add a row note to Table 1 and a sentence to section 4 stating that the 2011 land, livestock and crop windows fall in the Millennium Drought and the 2025 windows include the 2020-21 to 2022-23 La Niña seasons, that the 2011 three-year subsets all lie inside the drought window, and that the rise therefore includes a weather component the paper does not separate.
2. Make section 6, Table S11 and Supplementary Section S1 describe the same livestock-yield denominator, and add a sentence to section 12 saying the rule that pasture cannot carry crops was not tested on arable grazing land in the mixed farming zone, so the partition loss there may be overstated.
3. Add one sentence to section 6 citing Peters et al. (2016) on the cultivated against grazing-only land constraint, and correct the S4 single-year paragraph and the section 8.1 "lies between them" sentence as set out in section 3 above.

## 6. Is a further round needed?

No further referee round is needed. The changes are textual and checkable by the editor against this list; none requires a new model run.
