## What the web searches did show

1. **Journal version, 2000.** Simpson, R., Petroeschevsky, A. and Lowe, I. (2000), "An ecological footprint analysis for Australia", Australian Journal of Environmental Management 7(1), 11-18. DOI page: https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/14486563.2000.10648479 (publisher abstract page; full text is paywalled by the publisher, not verified as open). A ResearchGate record also exists: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/29454669_An_Ecological_Footprint_Analysis_for_Australia (not opened). This is the most likely route to the same content as the 1998 report. The abstract states a footprint of about 6 hectares per capita, more than four times the global fair share.
2. **Earlier draft.** One search summary mentioned a 1997 draft with the exact title "The Ecological Footprint of Australia, with a Focus on the South-East Queensland Region", Griffith University. The source page for that was not identified in the results, so the 1997 date, the "draft" status and the publisher are unverified. A second source fragment cited "(1998 Simpson et al." with no further detail.
3. **No other Griffith or Queensland EPA report** by these authors from 1997 to 2000 on Australia's footprint was found. A later regional study exists but is a different work: Graymore, M., "The Ecological Footprint of South East Queensland 2000-01", Coastal CRC / Australian School of Environmental Studies, Griffith University (cited in an Australian Parliament submission, https://aphref.aph.gov.au/house/committee/environ/cities/subs/sub177.pdf). It is not by the Simpson group.
4. **1999 Queensland State of the Environment report.** Catalogue snippets confirm the report exists (Brisbane: Environmental Protection Authority, 1999, with an executive summary and a companion volume). No search result showed its footprint section or any reference to Simpson, Petroeschevsky, Lowe or Griffith. Whether it cites the 1998 report is unconfirmed.

## How Murray would obtain them

1. **Search by hand first** (a normal browser gets past the bot walls): Trove, then catalogue.nla.gov.au, then Griffith Research Online, with the exact title in quotes and "Petroeschevsky" as a second term. The surname is rare and gives clean hits. Note the call number or holding library from any record.
2. **Library request.** If Trove or the NLA lists a holding library, request a copy or scan through that library's document-supply service. Grey literature like this is usually held at the NLA, State Library of Queensland, or Griffith's Logan or Nathan campus library.
3. **Interlibrary loan.** Through any university or public library, using the Trove record's identifier. The NLA copy service handles scans of short reports.
4. **Author contact.** Ian Lowe (Griffith University, later emeritus professor; also past president of the Australian Conservation Foundation) is the most reachable author and may hold a PDF. Request the 1998 report and ask whether it differs from the 2000 journal paper.
5. **Fallback.** Buy or access the 2000 journal article from the publisher (DOI link above) for the same method and result, if the report cannot be found.
6. **Canberra report.** Try the ResearchGate record in a browser (it may offer the file or a request-copy button), then ask CSIRO's library or the ACT Commissioner for Sustainability and the Environment.
