# Referee report, third round: Paper A (results), Population and Environment

Referee standpoint: population and environment research, demographic projections and resource constraints. Read: my second-round report, the response letter to round two, the revised manuscript (to the end of the declarations; the drafter-notes appendix was not considered), the supplement and the highlights.

## 1. Recommendation

Minor revision: the three points I set as conditions (a full regional robustness test, stated reference periods, and a paired test of the 2011 to 2025 change on one weather table) are now met in the text, and what remains is placement in the literature, an accurate scope sentence on projections, and the age-structure point, none of which needs new modelling of substance.

## 2. Second-round major points

**3.1 Reference periods and denominator. Partly answered.** Table 1 in section 2 gives every input's window and its distance from the population date, and the rule for a data year is stated; the text matches the letter. The window-centre check for 2025 (1.51 to 1.65 against 1.50) is useful and shows that the 30 June pairing gives the lowest margin; the text should say so in those words. Missing: the ERP release label is "not recorded", although Table S11 names the ABS Data API flow, so the vintage can be recovered and should be; and the 2011 check is declined because earlier populations are "not held", though the same ABS series runs back before 2011. Acceptable for publication if the vintage is stated.

**3.2 Definitional part of the land and livestock change. Answered.** Land and livestock are one layer in Table 3 and in the abstract and highlights, and section 4 gives an upper bound for the definitional part (about 0.19 million, 8% of the layer). The reclassification share itself is not estimated, which I accept given the combined layer. The external check is a FAOSTAT meat balance (section 6), not the output series I suggested, and section 1.4 still says "No annual national series of farm output across 2011 to 2025 was read"; that sentence now undersells the paper's own check and should point to section 6.

**3.3 Regional robustness on all choices. Answered.** The 19-choice draws run for all regions; sections 3, 9.1 and 10, Table 2, the abstract and conclusion 4 all say that no short-term flip is both robust and free of a flag, and "robust" is reserved for the full set. The text says what the letter says. The note that the North Tasmania and Mackay Whitsunday shares depend on equal weight to the two land sources is exactly right.

**3.4 The change between years. Answered.** Paired draws with common random numbers are reported in section 9.1, the abstract and conclusions open "at fixed settings", and the long-term change is called not established. The text matches the letter.

**3.5 Weather tables and the drought factor. Answered.** Table 7 puts both years on one table and section 8.1 says the 0.89 to 0.95 move was the table. Record-start variants are in Table S13, including 1990-91, which gives a milder 1-in-10 factor (0.762); the main text names only the 1950-51 and 1970-71 starts and should name 1990-91 as well, since that was the climate-trend question. The single failure probability is replaced by a range and is out of the conclusions. The 1-in-100 factor still rests on two seasons (1941 and 1915); the 1-in-20 figure in section 7.1 gives the reader a bound, which I accept.

**3.6 Projections. Partly answered.** (a) Capacity is said to be held at 2025 data; (b) the 2.6 times figure for 2062-63 is stated. (c) Official low and high variants are not mentioned in the letter or the text. (d) Regional projections are declined with the sentence "Regional projections are out of scope: no official series is published for these 53 regions." That reason is not accurate as worded: state governments publish projections at LGA or SA2 level that could be allocated as the ERP is. Declining is acceptable; the stated reason is not.

**3.7 Age-sex composition. Not answered in the paper.** Supplementary Section S10 gives a useful result: the age-sex weighted requirement of the 2010 population is 8.30 MJ, 1.038 times the model's 8.0 MJ, so both years' capacities run about 4% high. The main text does not mention it. The change from 2011 to 2025 is not computed because age-sex populations "are not held"; national ERP by age and sex at both dates is published by the ABS, so this reason is weak for the national figure, though acceptable for the regions. The non-resident population sentence the letter promises ("not modelled") is not in section 12.

**3.8 The scale curve as a partition effect. Answered, with one gap.** Section 5 is retitled, says "The loss is a partition effect, not an effect of size", uses one yield basis for the curve and gives the own-yield bound once. The dairy-on-cropland test (37.4% to 40.3%, Section S2) partly quantifies the rule. The sensitivity in which pasture carries some crops is not reported; acceptable, but the opening of section 5 should then say the dependence on the pasture rule is argued, not measured.

**3.9 Literature. Not answered; not acceptable.** The letter says Cohen, Peters et al. (2016), Zumkehr and Campbell (2015), Fader et al. (2013) and Gerten et al. (2020) were read and left out for word count. A paragraph of about 150 words can be found by trimming section 1.3 or 1.4, and a population journal's readers need it to place the result.

**3.10 Presentation. Largely answered.** Release names are gone from captions, one ratio is used, figures are rounded, section 11 is one table. Still open: the eight figures are placeholders, so I still cannot judge the regional maps; footnote 1 cites "ERRATA.md in the repository"; the draft header line and the [CONFIRM] flags in the declarations remain.

## 3. New problems introduced by the revision

1. Abstract and conclusions: "a ten-year diet window gives 39.1 million for 2025 (39.7 unrounded)". Two values for one quantity, separated by rounding of the release's diet inputs, invite the question whether the 41.5 million headline also carries a rounding effect of this size. Report one figure and state whether 41.5 is on rounded inputs.
2. Section 8.1: "above the population in every variant (on the leave-one-out reference one variant, 27.4 million, falls 0.6% below it)". The parenthesis contradicts the main clause; say "in every variant but one".
3. South West Queensland: section 3 gives "lowest capacity 15,500", section 9.1 "runs from 17,200 to 24,800". Label the second as the 5th to 95th percentile.
4. Section 5.3 now reports random groupings into seven groups, while the comparison with the 53 regions at 53 units (68 to 81%) has moved to Section S2. The main text's "the 53 regions 33%" sits beside a seven-group median with no like-for-like comparator; add the 53-unit range in one clause.

## 4. Minor points still open

- Second-round minor 6: the number of straddling SA2s and the population allocation moves are "not recorded"; both are a short computation.
- Minor 19: references and declarations still in Elsevier form (CRediT heading, "Declaration of generative AI ... in the writing process").
- Section 1.4 sentence on farm output series (see 3.2).
- Section 5.2: "28,609 on its own yields" against the three-figure rule.
- Highlights: none says the results are for a closed model with no trade; the fifth does not say the loss is a partition effect.
- I accept the authors' stands on minor 20 and 21.

## 5. Three changes before acceptance

1. Add to section 1.2 a paragraph of at most 150 words citing Cohen, Peters et al. (2016), Zumkehr and Campbell (2015), Fader et al. (2013) and Gerten et al. (2020) and saying where this paper's results sit among them, cutting the same length from sections 1.3 and 1.4.
2. In section 8.1 replace "no official series is published for these 53 regions" with "state government projections were not aggregated to these 53 regions", and add that official low and high series exist and were not used.
3. In section 2 add one sentence from Supplementary Section S10 stating that the age-sex weighted requirement is 1.038 times the model's 8.0 MJ, so both years' capacities are about 4% high, that the change in age structure from 2011 to 2025 was not computed, and that non-resident populations are not counted.

## 6. Further round

No further referee round is needed. The remaining items are textual and the editor can check them against this list; the new problems in part 3 are corrections of wording, and the substantive analyses I asked for in round two are now in the paper and match the letter.
