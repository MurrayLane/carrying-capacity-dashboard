# B66b readings: Paper A section 11 (livestock, food waste, grain reserves)

Ten papers read at targeted depth. Every file was opened and matched to its register row (title, authors, year) as the first step. Page numbers are the printed pages of each paper; Ananda is numbered 1 to 9 in its PDF; the Malau-Aduli text has no pages, so sections are named. The characterisation held in 6 rows (L157, L160, L161, L162, L163, L167), held in part in 3 (L156, L158, L166), and did not hold in 1 (L225, which held only a filing placeholder).

## Papers

**L156, Mayberry 2019.** A CSIRO study of greenhouse gas emissions from the Australian red meat sector, with 2005 and 2015 baselines (124.1 and 68.6 Mt CO2e) and six pathways to 2030. The herd it holds is about 25 million cattle, 70 million sheep and 0.5 million goats (p. 14). Productivity enters through the feedlot pathway and through trend figures for 1990 to 2010 that the authors quote from Wiedemann 2015a. So the line holds in part: herd assumptions yes, a productivity study no. It is an emissions and cost paper.

**L157, Wiedemann (file named Weidermann2015).** A life cycle assessment of seven feedlots in Queensland, New South Wales and Victoria, using feedlot records for 2007 and 2008. Per kilogram of liveweight gain it reports greenhouse gas, fossil energy, arable land and fresh water for short-fed, mid-fed and long-fed cattle (Table 9, p. 1155), and daily gain and feed conversion ratio (Table 1, p. 1151). Feed conversion ratio explains 0.95 of the variation in emissions per kilogram (p. 1156). The long-fed class is one feedlot. The characterisation holds. The paper is in the 2017 volume, online 2016.

**L158, Malau-Aduli 2022.** A literature review of the northern Australian beef cycle, from maternal nutrition to carcass quality, written to list research gaps. Phosphorus deficiency is one theme of several, so the register line is narrower than the paper. It gives a national herd of 23 million head and a northern share of 49% in the highlights but about 60% in the introduction. It reports no herd productivity rates. Holds in part.

**L160, Reutter 2017.** Compares five ways of estimating Australian food waste by stage and food type. Household totals run from 1.46 Mt (bin audit scaled up) to 4.99 Mt (ABS-based) (p. 162). The paper does not mention the National Food Waste Strategy. The characterisation holds; the Strategy link is the register's own context.

**L161, Mirosa 2020.** A handbook chapter reviewing Australian and New Zealand food waste research and policy. For Australia it gives 7.3 Mt in 2016-17 from the 2019 Baseline, with households 2.5 Mt (34%) (p. 229), and the November 2017 Strategy (p. 228). It also carries 7.3 Mt for 2008 and 7.5 Mt for 2008-09 (p. 226). Holds. The chapter gives no 50% target.

**L162, Ananda 2023.** An online survey of 4,324 Australian households in June 2019 asking what was thrown away in the past 7 days, in cups or slices. Planning and storing routines go with less waste, disposal routines with more (Table 4, p. 7), and the model explains little (R-squared 0.065, p. 6). The amounts are self-reported, with no national tonnage. Holds.

**L163, Spang 2019.** A review of food loss and waste measurement, drivers and solutions. It reports that definitions differ and data gaps remain (pp. 118-121), and cites FAO 2011 for 1.3 Gt, one third of food, from 2007 production (p. 130). It has no Australian national figure. It describes SDG 12.3 as halving retail and consumer waste per person (pp. 118-119). Holds.

**L166, Han 2024.** A four-page Nature Food Comment, not a research article. It proposes cropland reserves in China held in rotation or fallow and returned to grain when supply is short, alongside grain stockpiles (pp. 535, 537). It gives 78 Mha of marginal land and about 100 Mt a year of added grain capacity (p. 535). The register's "alternative to grain stocks" holds in part: the paper treats reserves as working with stockpiles.

**L167, Timmer 2024.** A book chapter on how national grain reserves and trade work together in food crises, using rice in 2007-08 and grain in 2022. It sets out four price models (pp. 231-232) and a household hoarding sum: 7 Mt of extra rice stock, a quarter of annual rice trade, lifts the price 167% (p. 234). It gives no stock level for any country. Holds.

**L225, Wright and Cafiero 2011.** A World Bank funded study of grain reserves for the Middle East and North Africa. It argues that holding stocks costs less than self-sufficiency or foreign land in dry countries, and that reserve size depends on supply diversity, supplier reliability and cost (pp. 71-72). The region imports a net 58.1 Mt of cereal (p. 61). One or two years' supply appears as a Saudi Arabia example (p. 72). The row held no characterisation, so "no".

## Flags

1. **Paper A section 11, waste measure ("Avoidable waste at the national target (6%)" in `papers/A_results/DISCUSSION_MEASURES.md`).** Spang pp. 118-119 describes SDG 12.3 as halving per-person retail and consumer waste and reducing early-stage losses. The 6% setting halves only the retail and consumer avoidable share (12% to 6%), while `analysis/waste_2025/README.md` states the national target as whole-chain. The section should say which scope the 6% follows. Mirosa p. 228 gives the Strategy aim as a significant reduction by 2030 with no 50% figure, so it is not the source for halving. (L161, L163)
2. **SCOPE.md E4 and the A11.3 baseline wording.** Mirosa p. 229 gives the 2019 Baseline as 7.3 Mt for 2016-17. SCOPE.md E4 calls 7.6 Mt "the national baseline"; `analysis/waste_2025/README.md` gives 7.6 Mt as the 2018-19 update. Name the base year with every figure, because the chapter also has a 7.3 Mt for 2008. The chapter is a printed source for the 7.3 Mt and the stage shares that the README says it could not reach. (L161)
3. **Paper A section 11 and Paper B section 6 ("How waste is treated").** Adds a caution only: Reutter p. 162 finds household totals differing by about 3.4 times across methods, so the 12% avoidable-waste setting, checked against one baseline, carries that spread. No figure in either outline changes. (L160)
4. **Paper A section 11, A11.4.** The register's "alternative to grain stocks" for Han does not hold; the Comment sets cropland reserves beside stockpiles (pp. 535, 537). No line in the two outlines cites it; use "complement". (L166)
5. **Paper A section 11, grain store rows of `DISCUSSION_MEASURES.md` (one year +48%, two years +64%).** None of the three grain papers gives a store size for Australia. Wright and Cafiero's "one or two years" (p. 72) is a Saudi Arabia case; Timmer gives no stock level; Han is about land. The store scenarios rest on the model. (L167, L225, L166)
6. **Register only, L157.** The register key says 2016; the paper is in volume 57 (2017), online 5 July 2016, and Mayberry cites it as 2017 (p. 21). The year in the key needs a decision.
7. **Register only, L158.** The northern share of the national herd is 49% in the Highlights and about 60% in the Introduction; name the section if either is quoted.
8. **Lead, L156.** The 1990 to 2010 productivity trend (liveweight gain 0.55 to 0.72 kg per head per day; weight at processing 538 to 574 kg; age 2.34 to 2.18 years, p. 14) is quoted from Wiedemann et al. 2015a, Agric. Syst. 133:109-118, which is not in the register. Cite that paper if section 11's "Livestock output +10% / +20%" is read against a past trend.
