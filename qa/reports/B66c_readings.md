# B66c readings: Paper A section 11, drought resilience, freight, import dependence

Eleven rows read at the TARGETED depth: abstract, introduction, results summary, conclusions and the tables or figures each characterisation rests on. Every file is the paper its row names. Characterisations: 9 hold, 2 hold in part (L170, L175), none fail. Page numbers are each paper's own printed pagination; Hunter (L176) has no printed page numbers, so PDF page positions are used; the Ritzel text rendering (L178) has none, so section names are used.

**L168, Botterill 2021.** A history of the National Drought Policy from 1992 to the December 2018 National Drought Agreement. The stated principles, farmer self-reliance and risk management, stay close to unchanged (Table 1, p83), while large spending is announced during severe drought (abstract, p77). The Agreement names a Future Drought Fund as a Commonwealth task (p79); the Fund has a target balance of AU$5 billion from AU$3.9 billion of initial investment (p81). Farm Management Deposits held over AU$5.6 billion in nearly 47,000 accounts at 31 May 2020 (p84). The row's characterisation holds.

**L169, Hughes 2022.** An ABARES paper that builds a farm-profit drought indicator from the farmpredict model and survey data, and compares it with rainfall and soil-moisture indicators and with farmers' own drought assessments. Farmers in 2019 are less likely than in 1993 to say they are in drought at a given rainfall, most in south-eastern and south-western cropping regions (pp6, 10). Mean drought frequency 1992-93 to 2018-19 is 17% by the rainfall and profit indicators and 21% by farmers (p7). The characterisation holds.

**L170, Hughes and Lawson 2017.** A book chapter that adjusts cropping-farm productivity for climate, 1977-78 to 2014-15. The climate effect on productivity fell from the mid-1990s; the text places the fall mainly in southern Australia (p187). Table 8.3 (p189) gives -4.71% for all cropping from 2000-01 to 2014-15, with Western Australia -7.63%, Queensland -4.92%, New South Wales -4.78%, Victoria -4.67% and South Australia -1.20%. The characterisation holds in part: the south-only reading is narrower than the table.

**L171, Sysak and Beilin 2017.** A handbook chapter on the local social effect of national drought policy, using two irrigation-district towns in northern Victoria, Cohuna and Rochester, studied 2009 to 2011 (p412). It reads drought policy together with water policy (abstract p405; conclusions p418). Victorian raw milk output fell 1.6 billion litres from 2001-02 to 2009-10, 1.3 billion of it in northern Victoria (p416). The characterisation holds.

**L172, Higgins 2018 (Computers and Electronics in Agriculture).** Describes TraNSIT, CSIRO's freight model for agriculture and forestry, covering over 30 commodities and 98% of volume moved by road and rail (abstract p187). Baseline annual road and rail transport cost is A$6.8 billion, 9.8% of farm gross value of production in 2015-16 (p196). A 2016 flood case adds about 5% to transport cost (p198). The characterisation holds; the register's "title only" is replaced by these figures.

**L173, Higgins 2018 (Rangeland Journal).** Applies TraNSIT to the A$100 million 2016 Beef Roads Program: 60 road upgrade submissions costing over A$3 billion (abstract p341). Northern supply chains run over 1000 km to markets on a largely unsealed network cut by seasonal flooding (p341); the introduction gives often over 3000 km and the summary up to 4000 km (pp341, 348). Using TraNSIT gave 72% more modelled transport saving than allocation without it (pp347, 350). The characterisation holds.

**L174, Davis 2020.** A scoping review of 325 studies of how environmental shocks enter food supply chains and how far they spread or fade. Studies centre on maize, wheat and rice (37%) and other cereals (10%), on precipitation (41%) and temperature (22%) extremes, and on production: 89% include it and 73% study only it; 6% examine spread between chain steps (p56). Responses named are substitute foods, source switching and strategic reserves (p54). The characterisation holds.

**L175, Kummu 2020.** National indicators for four nutrient groups, 1987-89 to 2011-13. Supply diversity rose for most of the world population (5.4 billion for protein, 5.7 billion for vegetables and fruit) while import dependency rose for a majority (p6). Import partners fell for 33% of people against 31% rising for protein, but rose for 44% against 40% for vegetables and fruit (p6). Australia appears among major exporters with falling protein production diversity (p4). The characterisation holds in part: "fewer import partners" is true for protein only.

**L176, Soliman Hunter and Taylor 2021.** A law and policy paper arguing that Great Australian Bight oil would not improve liquid fuel security, because refineries fell from seven in 2011 to two in 2021 (PDF p12) and capacity from 734,000 to 454,000 barrels a day, 2008 to 2018 (PDF p9). Oil stocks equalled 61 days of net imports in April 2020 against a 90-day IEA obligation (PDF p11). It quotes the National Farmers Federation on diesel at about 84% of on-farm energy use (PDF p13). The characterisation holds.

**L177, Wood 2018.** A brief communication using FAO food balance data (average 2007-2011) for eight nutrients, comparing actual supply with a no-trade scenario. Supply could meet the needs of an extra 16.79 billion people for vitamin B12 and 11 billion for protein, folate barely (70 million) (p34). Trade nourishes 146 to 934 million people in certain countries, depending on nutrient (p34). The authors call the no-trade case not realistic (p36). The characterisation holds; "title only" is replaced.

**L178, Ritzel 2024.** Builds an import vulnerability index from an exporter index (governance, climate risk, agricultural capacity, self-sufficiency) and a product index (exporter concentration, price volatility, net import dependency), weighted by robust principal component analysis, and applies it to Switzerland, 14 product groups, 2014 to 2019. The overall index ranged from 0.86 (2016) to 0.88 (2017); oilseeds scored highest at 1.07 (Section 4.3). The authors leave out imported inputs such as seeds, fertilizers and energy (Conclusion). The characterisation holds.
