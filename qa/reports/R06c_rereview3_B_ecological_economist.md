# Referee report, third round: Paper B, "Well-known carrying-capacity methods tested on common Australian data"

Referee B (ecological economics). I read my second-round report, the response letter, the revised paper, the supplement and the highlights, concentrating on the sections the letter names. I ignore the drafter notes.

## 1. Recommendation

Minor revision: the substantive requests are met or declined with reasons I can accept, and what remains is mostly wording that still claims a little more than the new analysis shows (the pairing as "sufficient", the model as "well suited" for food security, and a symmetry between lines (a) and (b) that section 5.11 itself has given up).

## 2. My second-round major points

**M1 (footprint reading in world-average hectares). Answered, with one inconsistency.** Section 5.11 now says "Line (b) is the footprint's own accounting; line (a) is a counterfactual", Tables 3, 5 and S11 label the world-average column a counterfactual, the abstract gives 146 to 343, and section 5.4 says crop and pasture "are summed as world-average hectares, with no equivalence factor". The letter and text agree. Two things remain. First, the abstract (opening of its third sentence on shares) and section 9.1 still present the two lines as equal alternatives ("depends on whether the productivity basis counts as input or method"), and 5.11 keeps "Which line holds is a classification, not a finding". If line (a) is a counterfactual, line (b) is the comparison and line (a) the sensitivity; the text should say so where the numbers are given. Second, the pooled reading is described as "the footprint's own accounting", but S3.11 shows it is the consumption-weighted mean of the land-type ratios with no equivalence factor. National footprint accounts, as I understand them, pool land types with equivalence factors, so the by-land-type readings are the footprint's accounting and the pooled one is this study's aggregation.

**M2 (national accounts). Not answered; acceptable with a narrower claim.** S2.5 gives the reason ("the public release has no Australian row, and the licensed data were not sought") and Table 3 adds daggers. I had understood Australia to be in the public national accounts; the author should name the edition and date checked. Since the reading is declined, the paper should say once, in 4.5 or 8.6, that its footprint results concern the 1990s food-row recipe and are not a reading of the current accounts.

**M3 (production-to-consumption ratios). Answered.** Section 5.11 states the algebra ("the population times the ratio of Australian output to consumption in world-average hectares (build 1: 2.47 pooled)"), S3.11 gives the land-type ratios, and the binding-land-type reading (53.9 to 77.9) now sits beside the pooled one in 5.10 and Tables 3, 5 and S11. The bridge (Table 7, panel B) is the decomposition I asked for. See section 3 below on how it is then summarised.

**M4 (the model's roles). Partly answered; acceptable.** Table 6 leads with reference-free measures; Table 9 has one model column, is called the author's judgement, and section 7.1 says "the table is not offered as a finding". S7 now discloses that the purpose definitions were written by the AI second rater and that the first is "what the model does by construction". Declining outside raters is acceptable once no finding is drawn, but two sentences still draw one (see section 3).

**M5 (robustness). Answered.** Table S5 and section 5.11 give the variants I asked for; the text and letter agree (-1% to 7%, 28% to 46%, 23% to 53% across references; 5.9% and 40.9% without fish).

**M6 (requirement and waste). Answered.** Line (c) is in 5.11, Table 6 and Table S7, with the band 10% to 16%. The 1.51 factor is applied to footprint and HALF readings that are on a mass basis (Table S10); a sentence should say the conversion assumes land per person scales in proportion to food energy.

**M7 (section 6). Answered, one request declined acceptably.** Table 8 has three panels, bundles and the upper bound are marked, the ladder is gone, and the abstract and highlight 5 now state the weather and waste changes one at a time. Not running the model with exports is acceptable given section 8.2; panel B's footprint 2.47 beside the proportional 2.41 makes my M3 point.

**M8 (window and land source). Partly answered.** S3.14 shows crop output 21% above its ten-year mean, the model +6% at most, crops alone 41.4. The land-source answer (ABARES raster counting 17.3% more cropland) resolves my question and corrects my premise. What is missing is the other side: "the output-scaled readings of the other methods were not rerun on the ten-year window" (S3.14). The cropland-bound footprint, the pooled footprint, HALF at own productivity and the energy balance all scale with crop output, so their multiples of the model are likely overstated for an average window. A proportional rescale (S3.14 already gives the crop factor 0.932 in yield and +20.8% in production) is quick and should be reported, at least for the primary readings in Table S11.

**M9 (literature). Not answered; partly acceptable.** The citing rule of section 3.1 is a coherent reason. The tie to van den Bergh and Rietveld (2004) in the introduction is one sentence, and the word limit is not a sufficient reason to omit it.

## 3. New problems introduced by the revision

1. **The pairing as "sufficient".** The abstract says "A bridge from HALF to the model spreads the gap over seven features; relaxing the pasture-crop pairing alone closes it", and highlight 4, 7.6 and 9.1 repeat "sufficient cause". The bridge itself gives the pooling rule 6.3 of 34.5 million, "18% of the gap when land is pooled in HALF's own currency" (5.14). Sufficiency rests on variant 1, which substitutes at k = 91.3 and lowers protein from 93 to 81 g a day; S3.12 shows that at that exchange rate the HALF end itself moves to 113.6. So the pairing closes the gap only in the energy-substitution currency, and the abstract reads as two contradictory sentences.

2. **The model "well suited" for food security.** Section 5.8 now says "the failure view does not cover the binding constraint", and 6.6 that "almost all of the fall comes from applying a cereal factor to pasture-fed output, which no livestock series has tested". Yet Table 9 rates the model "well suited, for crops" for purpose 1 and the failure view well suited in its note, and 7.6 advises "For a security claim, report a level and a failure probability from the model". In Australia the binding constraint is pasture, so on the paper's own rubric this is partly suited.

3. **Section 7.3** credits the model with explaining the 100 million through "a proportional count of exports", which section 6.1 says is a ratio between methods, not a model run.

4. **"Which line holds is a classification, not a finding"** (5.11) now conflicts with "line (a) is a counterfactual" two paragraphs earlier (M1 above).

## 5. Three changes before acceptance

1. In the abstract, highlight 4, 7.6 and 9.1, replace "sufficient cause" and "relaxing the pairing alone closes it" with a statement that the bridge attributes 18% of the HALF-to-model gap to the pooling rule in world-average hectares, and that relaxing the pairing closes the whole gap only when food is substituted at equal energy, with protein falling from 93 to 81 g a day.
2. In Table 9, the note and 7.2 and 7.6, rate the model and its failure view "partly suited" for food security and drought risk, giving as the limit that the weather factor is untested on pasture-fed output, which binds.
3. In the abstract, 5.11 and 9.1, present line (b) as the comparison and line (a) as a counterfactual sensitivity, delete "Which line holds is a classification, not a finding", and add to S3.14 and Table S11 the primary readings of the footprint, HALF and the energy balance rescaled to the ten-year output mean.

## 6. Is a further round needed?

No. The remaining changes are wording and one proportional rescale that the editor can verify against this list; the analysis behind them is already in the supplement, and none of the declined requests now undermines a claim the paper makes, provided the three changes above are made.
