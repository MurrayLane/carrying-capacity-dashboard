## 2. Quotations

### Productivities and yields used in the calculation

- p. 66 (the core formula): "average annual productivity or yield ['p,' in kg/ha]: aa<sub>i</sub> = c<sub>i</sub> / p<sub>i</sub>"; p. 65 defines c: "dividing average annual consumption of that item as calculated above ['c,' in kg/capita] by its".
- p. 66: "Most of our footprint estimates are based on average national consumption and world average land yields. This is a standardization procedure that facilitates "general case" comparisons among regions or countries."
- **p. 85, the only statement on food-land productivity:** "The second step in Ecological Footprint analysis involves converting consumption to a corresponding land area for each consumption category. This requires that we know the ecological productivity for each land-use category. We use trade and productivity figures compiled by the UN Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) to determine global average productivity for croplands. The productivity and carrying capacity of pasturelands was estimated from agricultural handbooks. Average forest productivity was set at 2.3 cubic metres of usable wood fibre per hectare per year. This corresponds to the average productivity of temperate Canadian forests,<sup>20</sup> and is also close to the 2 cubic metres per hectare per year used by the Dutch *Friends of the Earth* for analyzing global carrying capacity constraints.<sup>21</sup> As discussed above, we account for CO2 sequestration from the burning of fossil fuel at a land-for-energy ratio of one hectare per 100 gigajoules."
- p. 83, Table 3.3 abbreviations: "e) PASTURE = pastures for dairy, meat and wool production." "f) FOREST = prime forest area. An average roundwood harvest of 163 [m3/ha] every 70 years is assumed." (163 over 70 is 2.33 m3 a hectare a year, the same as p. 85.)
- p. 77: "Column C shows garden-land, the area used mainly for vegetable and fruit production. (Typically, this land has the highest ecological productivity.) Column D contains other cropland, and column E the pastureland used for dairy, meat and wool production."
- p. 81, Box 3.3: "Each Canadian consumes about 244 kilograms of paper every year. ... the production of each metric ton of paper in Canada currently requires 1.8 m3 of wood. For Ecological Footprint analyses an average wood productivity of 2.3 [m3/ha/yr.] is assumed."
- Energy, p. 72: "the consumption of 80 to 100 gigajoules of fossil fuel per year corresponds to the use of one hectare of ecologically productive land." "The most optimistic estimates for ethanol productivity suggest a net productivity of 80 gigajoules per year per hectare of ecologically productive land." "New Zealand tree plantations, at 12 tonnes of wood per hectare per year, ... would yield a land-for-energy ratio of 120 to 150 gigajoules per hectare per year. However, the productivities typical of Canadian, Russian or Scandinavian, forests would yield only 17-30 gigajoules per hectare per year (approximately 55-68 in the U.S.)."
- p. 73: "average forests can accumulate approximately 1.8 tonnes of carbon per hectare per year.<sup>8</sup> This means that one hectare of average forest can sequester annually the CO2 emission generated by the consumption of 100 gigajoules of fossil fuel." And: "one hectare of average forest could accumulate about 80 gigajoules of recoverable biomass energy per year in the standing timber."
- p. 74: "Therefore, we chose **one hectare per 1.8 tonnes of carbon emitted each year (one hectare per 100 gigajoules per year)** from the CO2 method as the land-for-energy ratio for fossil fuel. We use this ratio in all current EF assessments." (This is the 1.8 t C that B43 could not find; Simpson's attribution of it to the book holds.) Hydro: "a land-for-energy ratio of one hectare for each 1,000 gigajoules of continuous generating capacity would not be unreasonable"; "To date we have not included hydroelectricity consumption in our EF calculations."
- p. 65, Box 3.1 (sea, not counted): "average annual production is about 33.1 kg of fish per productive hectare or 0.03 hectares per kilogram of fish."
- p. 84 (food consumption, no land figure): "on average, each Canadian eats about 3,450 kilocalories worth of food each day, 1,125 in the form of animal products. Most of this food is produced by energy-intensive agriculture and is highly processed before it reaches the dinner table."
- p. 12 (the food land sum): "on average, 0.95 hectares of garden, cropland and pasture is needed for a typical Canadian." (0.02 + 0.60 + 0.33 on p. 82.)

## 4. Answer to question 3

**No.** The book, on these pages, states neither a pasture productivity (kg of meat, milk or wool a hectare, or hectares a tonne) nor the crop yields behind the food row. It states only their sources: FAO global averages for cropland and "agricultural handbooks" for pasture (p. 85), with the formula aa = c / p in kg/ha (p. 66). So Simpson's 1.758 ha of pasture and 0.312 ha of plant-food crop cannot be recomputed from the book with V07's 1991-92 inputs (58.4 kg of red meat a person; 89.2 kg of cereals as food, or the 620 to 650 kg of grain V07 found 0.312 would need). V07's fitted 33.2 kg of red meat a hectare is neither confirmed nor ruled out.

What the pages do add:

- The book's cropland rates are FAO global averages, the convention the project's rebuild follows. Its pasture rate is from a different kind of source (handbooks, "productivity and carrying capacity"), so a pasture rate unlike the FAO-derived 19.9 kg a hectare would not contradict the book. That makes a non-FAO rate behind Simpson's 1.758 plausible, but nothing here gives its value.
- Pasture is for "dairy, meat and wool" (pp. 77, 83), matching Simpson's key, not his text.
- The 1.8 t C a hectare that Simpson attributes to the book is on p. 73 and p. 74 (B43 open item closed).
- The book's consumption is trade-corrected (production plus imports minus exports, p. 65), with trade correction only for primary forestry, agriculture and energy products (p. 84). Simpson's "no need to consider imports and exports" fits this if his figures are apparent consumption, as ABS Table 1 is.
- Canada's food row is the only food land result: crop 0.60 (0.18 plant, 0.42 animal), pasture 0.33, garden 0.02 (p. 82); column totals crop 0.66, pasture 0.46 (p. 83).
