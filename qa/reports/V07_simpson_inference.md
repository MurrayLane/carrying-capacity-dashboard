# V07 checker report: inferring Simpson's conventions (Opus checker, read-only), 2026-11-07

**Verdict: not reproducible under stated conventions on the sources on hand.** Of the three food-row figures, only the animal-products feed crop (0.095) reproduces. The pasture figure (1.758) fits red meat only at 33.2 kg of meat per hectare, but that rate appears in no source on hand (Rees 1992, Rees 1996 and Wackernagel et al. 2002 give no pasture productivity; Wackernagel and Rees 1996, *Our Ecological Footprint*, and the 1993 report are not in the archive), is 1.67 times the FAO world ruminant-meat rate for 1990-92 (19.9 kg/ha), and contradicts the paper's own key, which puts dairy on pasture, and its text (60% of pasture for meat against the table's 67%). The plant-food crop (0.312) is 0.131 from food use at world yields (V04 0.142; project 0.107); total cropland rather than harvested area gives 0.167; reaching 0.312 would need about 620 to 650 kg of grain a person a year, which is the size of total grain use including feed (570 kg in 2021-23) but cannot be checked for 1991-92 (ABS 4306.0 Table 3 has no grain feed, seed or industrial use) and would double-count the feed already in the 0.095. The wool figure (0.854) cannot be checked: no wool consumption figure exists on hand (FAO world wool on pasture 0.97 kg/ha would imply 0.83 kg a person; Australia's own production rate 2.39 kg/ha, 2.04 kg).

**Project script against Simpson's conventions.** Follows: world-average yields, consumption basis, ABS Table 1, milk on pasture, a separate feed crop, wool excluded. Does not: shares pasture by edible protein (a rule Simpson never states); charges fruit and vegetables to crop where Simpson has a garden column (0.032); plant crop from food use only. The README's equivalence-factor idea (0.107 x 2.8) has no support in Simpson's text and should be dropped.

**2021-23 under alternative conventions** (red meat 35.6 kg a person; crop 0.213 with total grain use): plain FAO ruminant rate, pasture 1.207, together 256 million; the fitted 33.2 rate scaled by world change, pasture 0.724, together 388 million; project as it stands 0.860 and 343 million. So the capacity reading spans about 140 to 390 million across conventions.

**What would settle it.** The pasture productivity used by Wackernagel and Rees (the 1996 book or the 1993 report); 1991-92 Australian total grain use (ABARE commodity statistics may hold domestic use of wheat and coarse grains); Australian wool consumption; or the authors' worksheet.
