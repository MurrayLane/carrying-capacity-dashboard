## Paper A outline

### 1. Introduction (register labels A1.1, A1.4, A1.5)

- L152 (B67a): the 22.3% is a simulated effect of the 2001 to 2020 climate on 2015-19 farms, not a measured fall in profit, and its window starts in 2001, not 2011. Cite 22.6% (Table 5, p. 11) or "about 23%" and say "simulated"; give the 2050 range by pathway (-2 to -32% RCP4.5; -11 to -50% RCP8.5), not as one span.
- L152 beside L135 (B67a): Hughes gives a national wheat yield fall of 2.8 to 24.1% under RCP4.5 by 2050 (p. 16), against the under-10% from L135 Grundy in this section. Set the two side by side and do not merge them.
- L153 (B67a; also Paper B section 8): the 27% is a fall in simulated water-limited yield potential, not in harvested yield, which did not fall. Any line reading "wheat yields fell 27%" should read "yield potential". The series ends in 2015, so it gives context for the post-2011 window and does not measure it.
- L155 (B67a): the $53 billion is the present value of a modelled welfare loss and the paper states no discount rate. Cite it as the authors' model estimate.

### 7 and 8. The long term; Shocks and food security by region

- L154 (B66a): in the drought-by-return-period line (worst year of 5, 10, 20, 50, 100) and the narrative arc, Falster and others find the unusual feature of the 2017-2019 event was the three-year sequence (PDF pp. 6, 9). A single-year return-period reading does not describe it. If the section or A1.5 uses the event as a benchmark, state it as a three-year basin-wide event.
- L224 (B66a; also section 12 and Paper B section 6): the 22 million fed at home plus 40 million fed abroad (p. 30) comes from a government expert panel report with no base year. It is not on the same footing as the model's short-term capacity of 33.3 million for 2011 (the headline). Quote it only with its source and say the diet and export treatment differ.
- L192 (B67b; also section 11 `DISCUSSION_MEASURES.md` D7 and Paper B section 8): the paper covers post-farm manufacturing and distribution energy only. It has no farm-fuel figure, no liquid fuel per tonne-km in the main text (Appendix I is not on file) and no Australian figure. It supports a statement about post-farm energy, not the model's liquid fuel settings.

### 11. Discussion: what would widen the margin (register labels A11.1, A11.3 to A11.7)

- L156 (B66b): the 1990 to 2010 productivity trend (liveweight gain 0.55 to 0.72 kg per head per day; weight at processing 538 to 574 kg; age 2.34 to 2.18 years, p. 14) is quoted from Wiedemann and others 2015a, Agric. Syst. 133:109-118, which is not in the register. Cite that paper if "livestock output +10% / +20%" is read against a past trend.
- L159 (B67a): the 13% and 18% are industry forecasts of animal numbers taken as inputs (p. 398), from a 2020 flock low. The paper measures radiative forcing, not productivity, so it cannot support a productivity claim and the "nothing Australian" gap at livestock productivity stays open.
- L161 and L163 (B66b): the "avoidable waste at the national target (6%)" setting in `DISCUSSION_MEASURES.md` halves only the retail and consumer avoidable share (12% to 6%). Spang pp. 118-119 describes SDG 12.3 as halving per-person retail and consumer waste plus cutting early-stage losses, and `analysis/waste_2025/README.md` states the national target as whole-chain. The section should say which scope the 6% follows. Mirosa p. 228 gives the Strategy aim as a significant reduction by 2030 with no 50% figure, so it is not the source for halving.
- L161 (B66b): Mirosa p. 229 gives the 2019 Baseline as 7.3 Mt for 2016-17. `SCOPE.md` E4 calls 7.6 Mt "the national baseline", and `analysis/waste_2025/README.md` gives 7.6 Mt as the 2018-19 update. Name the base year with every figure, because the chapter also carries 7.3 Mt for 2008. The chapter is a printed source for the 7.3 Mt and the stage shares that the README says it could not reach.
- L160 (B66b; also Paper B section 6, "How waste is treated"): a caution only. Reutter p. 162 finds household totals differing by about 3.4 times across methods, so the 12% avoidable-waste setting, checked against one baseline, carries that spread. No figure changes.
- L166 (B66b): the register's "alternative to grain stocks" for Han does not hold. The Comment sets cropland reserves beside stockpiles (pp. 535, 537). No outline line cites it; use "complement".
- L167, L225, L166 (B66b): none of the three grain papers gives a store size for Australia. Wright and Cafiero's "one or two years" (p. 72) is a Saudi Arabia case, Timmer gives no stock level, and Han is about land. The grain store rows of `DISCUSSION_MEASURES.md` (one year +48%, two years +64%) rest on the model alone.
- L170 (B66c): the register line "mainly in the south" follows the text at p. 187. Table 8.3 (p. 189) shows Queensland (-4.92%) and the northern GRDC region (-4.07%) close to New South Wales (-4.78%) and Victoria (-4.67%), Western Australia largest (-7.63%) and South Australia smallest (-1.20%). Cite state or region values.
- L175 (B66c; also Paper B section 8): the register wording "fewer import partners" holds for protein only. For vegetables and fruit, partners rose for the larger population share (p. 6). The finding is global; Australia is named among major exporters (p. 4) and in a cluster where production and supply diversity fell slightly (p. 9). Word any Australian use as the global pattern.
- L218 (B67b; also `DISCUSSION_MEASURES.md` C and D5): El Nino and positive Indian Ocean Dipole states drive poor wheat years across much of Australia at the same time. The paper names the mechanism that ties southern states' bad years together and can be cited beside the state worst-year factors. It changes no measured figure. Paper B section 8 (variability between years) is supported as written.

### 12. Limitations and conclusions (register labels A12.1 to A12.4)

- L184 (B66d): in the forced local self-sufficiency line, any use of Payen yields must say they are per growing cycle and that 42% of observations are controlled-environment.

- L183 (B67b; also `DISCUSSION_MEASURES.md` D9): the 15% is food energy for the 2.67 million residents of the 6032 modelled blocks, not Greater Sydney's 4.9 million (p. 5). - L188 (B66d): any sentence that subsidies reduced return flows should carry the authors' wording. The abstract states the finding; the body says return flows are not measured and the effect is conditional (pp. 497, 498, 505).
- L188 (B66d): p. 500 gives a cost ratio of at least 2.5, while the two Table 2 averages give 2.45. Quote the two prices (AUD 2,026 and AUD 4,970 per ML), not the ratio.
- L180 (B67a; also Paper B section 8): no change to a claim. The 11 to 28% is a range across single crops, not an uncertainty range and not the whole diet, and it gives no Australian number.
- L224 (B66a): see section 7 and 8 above.

### 3. How the review was done, and the Type line (register label B3.1)

- L199 (B66e; also sections 1 and 4): the Qiu chapter is dated 2024 by publication but its data year is 2006 (p. 182) and it serves the 2006 to 2010 plan period (p. 151). A rule "literature since 2011" gives different answers by publication year and by data year. The selection statement should say which.
- L200 (B66e): Haddaway and others list lack of a protocol and of a comprehensive search as problems for reviews claiming rigour (pp. 1584-1585) and ask all reviews for transparency (p. 1583). Section 3 should state the selection rule and exclusions plainly.
- L201 against L203 (B66e): Greenhalgh and others regard how-to guides that require explicit search and strict inclusion criteria for narrative reviews as misleading (p. 3 of 6). Snyder asks for reasoned, transparent inclusion criteria in any approach (p. 337). Section 3 should say which it follows.
- L223 (B66e; also the Type line): Grant and Booth define "critical review" as a type and treat narrative as a style of synthesis (Table 1, p. 94). "Critical narrative review" combines Grant and Booth with Greenhalgh and others; cite each for its part.

### 4. A catalogue of methods (register labels B4.7, B4.8, B4.9)

- L208 and L209 (B66f): the register places both under integrated resource-flow or stocks-and-flows models (B4.8). Gao describes LUTO as an integrated, constrained, partial-equilibrium linear programming model (p. 217), so it is an optimisation model. Navarro Garcia states the FABLE Calculator uses no optimisation (Methods). The family label for LUTO needs a decision; nutrition optimisation (B4.7) is a separate family in the register.
- L215 (B66f): the paper's own citation line reads LI Yi et al. 2018 (p. 455), so the key "Yi 2018" uses the given name. A Paper B line citing it should read Li et al. 2018 (water-bounded capacity, B4.9). The register title is corrected.
- L204 and L206 (B66f): the two papers set different per-person water limits. Ridoutt uses 216 L a day for Australia (1,100 km3 a year globally, 70% to food, 9.7 billion people, p. 804); Chaudhary uses 786 L a day (1,980 km3 a year over the 2011 population, pp. 7696-7697). Any sentence quoting a per-person water limit must name the basis.
- L209 (B66f): the abstract says "around 10%", the Results give 13% (with exports required) and 41% (five environmental targets, no export requirement). A quoted share needs the Results figure and its target set.
