# B66d readings: self-sufficiency, urban agriculture, water

Seven rows read at targeted depth. Each file was opened and matched to the register row (title, authors, year) before reading. Page numbers are the printed pages of each paper; the CSV notes give the offset to the PDF sheet. Result: 5 characterisations hold, 1 holds in part, 1 row had none to test.

**L179 Clapp 2017.** A Viewpoint on what food self-sufficiency means. It defines the term as domestic production relative to domestic consumption, so trade stays possible (p. 89), gives the self-sufficiency ratio and a 2,500 kcal per person per day measure, and argues for a continuum between closed borders and fully open trade (Fig. 3, pp. 93-94). The register line holds. The numbers it quotes belong to other studies it cites (Porkka, Fader, FAO).

**L181 Schreiber 2021.** A systematic review of 42 foodshed studies from 829 screened, sorted into capacity, flow and hybrid types (pp. 1, 4). Capacity studies are most frequent; regional counts are North America 19, Europe 13, Asia 6, with one hybrid study in Australia (p. 9). The register line holds.

**L184 Payen 2022.** A meta-analysis of 200 studies and 2,062 yield observations. Urban yields are on par with or above FAO global average conventional yields for 2015 to 2020, stated per growing cycle, with 42% of observations from controlled environments. The register line holds; the basis (per cycle, not per year) is added. The file has no page numbers, so sections are named.

**L187 Prosser 2021.** A critical review of Murray-Darling Basin water management under climate change. It reports a median fall of about 20% in streamflow under 2 C warming (p. 4) and a modelled 56% average share of surface water consumed, above 70% in a quarter of years (p. 6). The register line holds.

**L188 Grafton and Wheeler 2018.** A review of water recovery to 30 September 2017. Buy-backs cost AUD 2,026 per ML against AUD 4,970 for infrastructure (p. 501); no basin-wide link is seen between recovery and Murray Mouth flows (r = 0.10, p. 504). The return-flow reduction is stated in the abstract; the body says return flows are not measured (p. 497) and the effect is conditional (p. 498). Holds in part.

**L189 Grafton and co-authors 2018.** A Science Policy Forum piece: higher irrigation efficiency rarely lowers basin-scale water consumption, with four cases (p. 749) and a five-step reform agenda (pp. 749-750). The register line holds, including the Australian co-authors.

**L222 Badami and Ramankutty 2015.** Tests whether built-up urban land could grow 300 g of vegetables a day for the urban poor in 19 countries. Medium case: under 3% of urban area in the high-income countries listed, over 100% in five low-income countries (Table 3, p. 13). Australia is not in the sample. The row had no characterisation, so new text is supplied.
