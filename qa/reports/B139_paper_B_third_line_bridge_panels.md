## Summary: line (c) first, then the bridge

**Line (c).** Expected direction, written in the script's docstring as the brief asks: wider. Result: wider. With the model's requirement (8.0 MJ a day as eaten) and its own loss factors charged to every method (every method reading x 1.512), harmonising closes **14.7%** of the log distance to the corrected model, against 38.5% on line (b); to the median of the method centres 20.0% (16.0% on line (b)); the spread of method centres narrows 26% (SD of logs 0.65 to 0.48; line (b) 0.44, 32%). The footprint and HALF still close 38% and 46% of their own distances; the energy balance (-22%), people per hectare (-5%) and the commodity balance (-19%) move away from the model. The share stays positive; it does not go below zero. Putting the model on the FAO supply requirement instead (3,525 kcal, waste inside; the model at 27.9 million, evaluator) gives 14.3% and leaves the methods and their spread as line (b). Charging no avoidable waste to anyone gives 21.0% (model 55.3) and 20.7% (model 31.0). **Lead with 8.0 MJ as eaten with the model's loss factors (c1):** it is a requirement, it leaves the model at its published 41.5, c1 and c2 agree to within half a point, and the waste choice (14 to 15% against 21%) matters more than the requirement choice.

**The bridge.** From HALF at Australian productivity (76.0 million; the machine gives 75.97) to the corrected model (41.5; the machine gives 41.5035, the evaluator's value to 1e-6), the net distance of 34.5 million divides, averaged over all 5,040 orderings of seven features (million people, from HALF toward the model; share of the total):

The largest feature is the diet (the model's guideline diet takes 9.2% of its energy from pasture-fed red meat, the food balance's diet 4.9%), then the pasture-fed yield layer, then the pool. **Pairing is a sufficient cause and one of several**: about a fifth of the net distance when land is pooled in HALF's own currency; 60% of a larger distance when food is substituted at the crop-equivalent energy rate (B114 variant 1, reproduced at 81.70). **The protein-held variant** (legumes and cereals replace pasture-fed red meat, energy and protein held at 93.1 g, every other group capped at 3 times its amount) reads **85.4 million** (84.9 and 86.2 at caps of 2 and 5), above variant 1's 81.7 and not below it: variant 1 substitutes the diet's cropland foods in their present proportions, including crop-fed meat and eggs, and lets protein fall to 80.9 g.

## 1. The third line (`harmonised_line_c.py`)

Construction. One factor multiplies every method reading: M1 = 14.75 MJ x 0.8202 / 8.0 MJ = 1.512 (the food balance's supply, the energy-weighted eaten share of its foods on the model's retail and consumer loss factors from `intake_variant.csv`, over the model's requirement), because each method's diet is the food balance mix and scaling it to the new energy cancels the item shares. The grain with embodied feed grain reading rises 10% (their 303 kg of feed grain is kept, as in B116). c3 uses M0 = 14.75 / 8.0 = 1.844. c2 and c4 raise the model's food amount to M1 and M0 and run the evaluator. Line (b) is rebuilt in the script and equals `harmonised_comparison_lines.csv` (38.5%, 16.0%, 17.2% to the three references; the script stops otherwise).

`harmonised_line_c.csv` has the columns of `harmonised_comparison_lines.csv` for line (b) and the four cells: the 19 readings, the method centres, the distance, share closed per method and for all five (to the model, to the median and to the geometric mean of the centres), and the dispersion measures. `harmonised_line_c_robustness.csv` repeats B135's nine core variants (base, each method out, fish dropped, pasture-alone footprint dropped, median centres) for line (b), c1 and c2:

Per method (share of its own distance closed, line (b) to c1): footprint 61% to 38%, HALF 68% to 46%, people per hectare 26% to -5%, energy and protein balance 8% to -22%, commodity balance -15% to -19%.

Flag for the coordinator: the records give the model's requirement as 8.0 MJ as eaten (nutrition README, `bases.csv`), while the diet columns the evaluator computes the model's land from sum to 7.376 MJ a day (the 0.62 MJ is drinks and other items outside the land accounting, per the nutrition README; not traced in the workbook). Line (c) uses 8.0 as the brief says; with 7.376 the factors are 1.640 and 2.000 and c2 and c4 read 25.8 and 28.7 (`harmonised_line_c_model_runs.csv`); no direction or order changes. B116's intake variant (the FAO diet at 2,892 kcal eaten, no waste charged) is a different construction: line (c) sets every need to the model's 1,912 kcal eaten with waste charged on both sides, so the two sets of multiples are not comparable and Paper B should not set them side by side without saying so.

## 2. The bridge (`bridge_half_to_model.py`)

One machine reproduces both ends: the model's eleven food groups at the evaluator's diet and the HALF world rebuilt from half_index.py (46 food balance items, 13.60 MJ a day, land split into cropland and pasture, converted to Australian m2 with kappa 0.876 and 0.218). Seven switches: the land pool, the requirement, waste, the diet composition, the cropland yield layer, the pasture-fed yield layer, non-food land. `bridge_half_to_model.csv` has, for each of four games, the feature, forward, reverse, all-orderings average, share, SD over orderings, the log-scale average and a note; `bridge_groups.csv` lists the two worlds group by group. Games: G1 primary (pool at world-average hectares, k = 4.01); G2 requirement and waste as one switch; G3 pool by energy substitution (k = 91.3, B114 variant 1); G4 pool by substitution with protein held (k = 145.6). The exact average is computed twice (over orders and by the subset-weight formula) and the two agree to 1e-9; the averages add to the total change; the end-point and B114 checks are rows of the csv and all pass.

What the records cannot separate (full list in the README): the requirement from waste (FAO supply holds the waste; separated only through the model's loss factors, so the pair is the robust number); the allocation rule, the data and the productivity conversion inside the pasture-fed yield layer; the pattern and the definition of food inside the diet composition; the protein floor (it binds only when food is substituted, so it appears only in G3 against G4); land base and population (not switches: 363.52 Mha agrees to 0.01 Mha and no population enters either capacity); exports (they enter through kappa and cannot be told from pasture productivity). G3 and G4 start from 113.6 and 116.9, not from HALF's 76.0 (the HALF world under that pooling); the difference is printed, not attributed.

## 4. The ten-year production window (`production_window_10y.py`)

The FAOSTAT files on disk hold Australia for 2011 and 2020 to 2023 only; the ten years 2014 to 2023 are not held (four of ten). The script uses the held years, says so in every row, and computes the ten-year rows when `fao_au_production_2014_2023.csv` exists. **Fetch for Murray:** `https://bulks-faostat.fao.org/production/Production_Crops_Livestock_E_All_Data_(Normalized).zip` (hash-pinned in `qa/fao_bulk_hashes.csv`, zip sha256 `c5835418c18f9322e7decbd6800f93a216eaae3cdfa31acb08f0518c0c6d6853`), then `extract_fao_au_production_10y.py` (commands and hash check in its docstring). Result on the years held: **2021 to 2023 is above the mean of the five held years (2011, 2020 to 2023) by 9.3% for crop production (median over eight groups; 4.2% for yield; cereals 5.2% and 8.9%) and by 3.3% for livestock production (median over six; beef -2.4%, sheep and goat meat +9.1%, milk -2.0%)**; against the four years 2020 to 2023 the figures are 5.3%, 1.4% and 0.5% (a weak test, the window contains 2021 to 2023). The model's short-term capacity with its yield layers rescaled to the five held years is 41.35 million (-0.37%; crops only 41.44, livestock only 41.41); to 2020-2023 41.63; to the held years outside 2021-23 40.73 (-1.9%); pasture binds, so the crop rows move it little.

## 5. The production comparison (`analysis/comparison_2025/production_comparison.py`)

The model's production inputs against FAOSTAT's for the same items and years (year rule typed first: winter field crops against the calendar year the ABS crop year starts, summer crops and horticulture against the year it ends). Land: 363.52 Mha both. **Field crops: 26 comparisons agree to 0.0002%** (FAOSTAT carries the ABS series; 94% of the compared tonnes). Fruit and vegetables: FAOSTAT's series is not the ABS series, median absolute difference 13.1%, up to 59.2% (almonds). Milk: within 0.1% to 3.5%. Wool: 2.8% above FAOSTAT's shorn greasy where FAOSTAT equals AWI (the base is the ABS 2009-10 total). Beef and sheep meat: ABS tonnes times a calibration (1.086 and 1.155) to the thesis's 2010 figure; the ABS tables are not on disk, so the model's 2022-23 to 2024-25 meat inputs cannot be recomputed; the archive's 2010 bases are 7.6% and 21.8% above FAOSTAT's 2011 (a one-year offset). Poultry, pigs and eggs: no production input (feed-grain yield over a conversion ratio). **The sentence that makes the data claim true:** "The methods and the model share a common land base (FAOSTAT's 363.52 Mha of cropland and pasture, which the ABS holdings under the model match to 0.01 Mha) and the methods share the FAO food balance for 2021 to 2023; the model keeps its own diet and its own production layer, which is ABS-based, equals FAOSTAT's for the field crops, lies within 4% of FAOSTAT's for milk, differs for fruit and vegetables, and carries a calibration to the thesis's meat figures that FAOSTAT does not (Table S6)." The abstract's "Australian data for 2021 to 2023 (FAOSTAT 2025; the model on its own 2025 layer)" and 5.1 carry it.
