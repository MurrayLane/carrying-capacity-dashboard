## What the paper is

An LCA of the national Australian beef herd (beef from the dairy herd and the live-export herd excluded), cradle to farm gate, functional unit 1 kg liveweight before processing (p. 110). The herd is modelled in five-year periods (1 July to 30 June) from ABS livestock and slaughter statistics and the ABARES farm survey, calibrated to ABS standing numbers and slaughter (pp. 111, 113). Feed intake from the Minson and McDonald model used in the national inventory (p. 111). Regions: national, north and south compared only for breeding efficiency (p. 114); State and Territory detail only for land clearing (Fig. 3, p. 114). Herd systems: grass-finished and grain-finished (feedlot) steers and surplus heifers (Table 1, pp. 111, 115). Years are five-year period averages, not single years: 1981 is 1981-85, 1990 is 1986-90 (or 1991-95), 2000 is 1996-2000, 2010 is 2006-10.

## Mayberry's second-hand quote (L156, p. 14)

It matches. Mayberry's 0.55 to 0.72 kg per head per day, 538 to 574 kg and 2.34 to 2.18 years are the 1991-95 and 2006-10 columns of Table 1 (p. 111), all three to the printed digit. Two points of reading: the paper gives period averages, so his "1990" is the 1991-95 average (the 1986-90 column gives 0.55 kg, 540 kg and 2.36 years); and his window skips the 1981-85 start (0.39 kg, 474 kg, 2.41 years) and the 2001-05 peak (0.76 kg, 597 kg, 2.20 years), after which the paper reports a slight decline (p. 113). His 0.75 million feedlot cattle in 1990 (3.4% of the herd) is not in this paper.

## Pasture or feed-conversion figure beside the model's livestock settings

None. The paper prints no feed conversion ratio, pasture yield, stocking rate, dry sheep equivalent or kilograms per hectare; "feed conversion" appears only in words (pp. 110, 115). Closest items: beef per breeding cow mated (301 to 396 kg, p. 113) and the north and south breeding efficiency (65% against 84%, ratio 0.77, p. 114), which sits near the north-south turn-off ratio of 0.73 to 0.81 in `analysis/pasture_2025/README.md`; the paper does not define breeding efficiency, so this is a loose cross-check only.

## Flag for the Paper A scope (section 11, A11.1 Livestock productivity)

* This is the Australian trend A11.1 lacked (B61 marked the point thin: nothing Australian). It gives the size of past gains: steer gain +85% and liveweight +21% (474 to 574 kg) over 30 years, beef per breeding cow +32%, carcase weight +13.5%. A +10% livestock output test is inside that range.
* The gain is mostly feedlot finishing: grass-finished steer gain rose under 20% and fell between the last two periods (p. 115). It came with fossil energy up 1.9 times and arable land for feed up from 0.2 to 1.7 million ha (p. 114). A11.1 should say a +10% output measure is not free of feed-land cost.
* Weaning rate shows no trend over 30 years (p. 114), and gains slowed after 2001 (p. 117); extrapolating the 1981 to 2010 slope would overstate what is available.
* Scope limits: beef only, cradle to farm gate; dairy beef (8 to 12%) and the live-export herd excluded (p. 110); ends in 2010, the same year the model's livestock output is calibrated to (`analysis/pasture_2025/README.md`).
* The authors' limits (p. 113): disparate and discontinuous datasets, no statistics on growth rates in slaughter cattle, especially grass-fed, no quantitative uncertainty analysis, extra caution for land occupation and land-use emissions.

## Other printed oddities

Abstract says land-use emissions fell "around 42%"; results and discussion say 41% (pp. 109, 114, 116). Total emissions printed in Gg (p. 113).
