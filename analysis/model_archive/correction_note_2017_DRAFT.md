## What was wrong

1. **Biodiesel yield.** The model counted the oil content of oilseed twice in the biodiesel yield (canola 14.8% where the source gives 35.2%; ABARE, *Australian Energy Resource Assessment* 2009, Table 12.12). It lowered the long-term capacity of every region that uses biofuel land.
2. **Beef carcase-to-retail yield.** Beef had a yield of 1.0 between carcase and retail cut where lamb, veal and pork have 0.894, 0.83 and 0.776. It raised the short-term capacity.
3. **State populations.** The state populations in the default inputs were below the populations of the states' own regions (Queensland 3.91 against 4.51 million, South Australia 1.31 against 1.64, Western Australia 1.89 against 2.29). The capacities were unaffected; the proportions of capacity in Table 2 and the over-capacity statements for states were.
4. **Settings.** Table 2 was produced with earlier settings than Table 1 states (timber 0.5 against 1 m3 a person, short-term biofuel 0.53% against 0.5%, long-term infrastructure 365 against 500 m2); the effect on national and state capacities is under 3.3%.

## Statements that change

* "Just over 40 million" (section 3.1 and abstract): **33.3 million**, 1.5 times the population of 22.3 million, not almost twice. The corroboration from exports of half the red meat and broadacre crops is weaker.
* "The Northern Territory and Tasmania are the only states deemed to be over capacity" (short term): **Victoria is also over capacity (104%)**.
* "All states, with only the exception of Western Australia, are considered to be currently over-capacity" (long term): **all states are over capacity; Western Australia is 117%**.
* "Only 13 regions are under-capacity" (long term): **17 regions**. The attribution to "the extra demands on productive land for biofuel" does not hold: with the correction biofuel is no longer the leading demand (setting biofuel to zero adds 0.6 million people, not several million).
* Figure 4 and section 4.2 ("carrying capacity only increases until the 7 percent mark of cropping land is reached"): **with the correction long-term capacity is 15.4 million at 4% cropping land and stays there to 10%**; cropland is not the constraint, so the figure and the paragraph describing it no longer hold. The statement that at 3.5% meat and eggs and 10% cropping the capacity reaches 23 million becomes 23.8 million.
* "Australia's long-term carrying capacity may be nine million people" (discussion): **15.4 million**.

## What does not change

The ordering of the main finding (short-term capacity exceeds long-term capacity, the largest populations are in regions over capacity, and the Murray-Darling Basin and south-west Western Australia hold the under-capacity regions) is not re-tested here region by region; the long-term level of the sum of regions rises from 3.8 to 4.3 million people.
