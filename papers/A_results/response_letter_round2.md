# Response to the second-round referee reports

**Manuscript:** Australia's carrying capacity 2011 to 2025 by region (Paper A, results), submitted to Population and Environment

Dear Editor,

Both referees recommended major revision. The revision answers most points by computation. Of the 20 numbered major issues, 14 are answered by a change to the paper, 2 by showing what the records hold where the premise does not apply, and 4 are out of scope, each with the reason given below. The three largest changes are these. First, the 19-choice Monte Carlo draws were run for every region; on them no short-term status flip is both robust and free of an artefact flag, and the two flips called clean on 13 choices are not robust. Second, 2011 and 2025 are paired on the choices they share: 2025 is above 2011 in all 2,000 paired draws, while the long-term change is not established. Third, land and livestock are one layer in the decomposition, and the drought results give both bounds, 30.8 million at a 1-in-10 year with the cereal factor on all output and 41.2 million on crops and grain-fed animals alone. The single failure probability of 5.8% is replaced by a range, 2.3% to 19.4% a year. Both years are on one weather table.

## First referee (population and environment)

I thank the referee for the report.

**3.1 "The two data years are not the years named."** Change. Section 2 carries Table 1: the reference period of each input and the years from each window's centre to the population date. It states the rule: "A data year pairs the production data of its window with the population to be fed at 30 June of that year." As a check, on window-centre populations capacity for the 2025 windows is 1.51 to 1.65 times the population (1.58 on the livestock window), against 1.50 at 30 June 2025. The same check for 2011 needs populations before June 2011, which are not held. Both years use one ABS series, estimated resident population at 30 June by 2021 SA2, shared among zones by residential land; its release label and retrieval date were not recorded (Supplementary Section S8). The national 2011 population is the regional sum, 22,336,514 (the thesis row was 22,326,388), so growth is 23.6% (was 23.7%). The 2025 figure of 27,608,200 is not rounded: it is the allocated SA2 sum, 304 below the ABS total.

**3.2 "Part of the land and livestock change is a change of statistical definition."** Change. Land and livestock are one layer in Table 3, adding 2.46 million to short-term capacity (the average over 40,320 orderings); the nine-layer split is in Supplementary Section S12. Abstract and highlights do not name livestock as a separate cause; the consumption and red-meat-mix layers add 5.6 million (68%). On the definition: giving the 2025 data the 2011 grazing area, with output and diet fixed, lowers capacity by 194,427 people, at most 8% of the 2.46 million (an upper bound, section 4). I have not estimated the share of the pasture fall due to the 2016 reclassification beyond that bound, and says the scope and frame changes after 2008-09 are not documented in the sources read. As an external check, section 6 sets FAOSTAT meat production over meat eaten, 1.57 in 2021-23 and 1.51 in 2010-12, beside the model's 1.50 and 1.49.

**3.3 "The regional robustness test omits the choices that dominate national uncertainty."** Change. The referee is right. The 19-choice draws run for all 53 regions. North Tasmania's 5th percentile is 148,681 (174,487 on 13 choices), against a population of 156,393; Mackay Whitsunday's is 135,581 against 153,841. North Tasmania is over capacity in 13.5% of draws and Mackay Whitsunday in 29.4%, shares that depend on the equal chance given to the two land sources. Of eight short-term flips none is both robust and flag-free; seven do not keep their 2025 status, and SA Arid Lands keeps it but is a land-definition case. Section 3 says: "'Robust' is used only for flips that pass on the full set." South West Queensland's long-term flip is robust, with a lowest draw of 15,477 against a population of 7,109. The abstract, sections 3 and 10 and conclusion 4 match.

**3.4 "Change between years is not tested."** Change. 2011 and 2025 are paired with common random numbers on the seven choices both years share. On those alone 2025 exceeds 2011 by 6.5 to 8.4 million (5th to 95th percentile); with the 2025-only data choices drawn for 2025 as well, by 2.3 to 16.5 million (1.9 to 9.6 on holdings land only); in no draw is it lower. The 13% of 2025 draws below 2011's base value is an unpaired comparison. The long-term change is not established: 26% of paired draws fall (52% on holdings land only). The abstract opens "at fixed settings".

**3.5 The two weather tables, and the drought factor.** Change. On the 2025 table the 1-in-20 margin is 0.95 in both years; 2011 on its thesis table is 0.89. The apparent improvement from 0.89 to 0.95 was almost all the table. Table 7 uses one table; the thesis-table rows are in Supplementary Table S14. Cereal records starting in 1950-51, 1970-71 or 1990-91, and area-weighted variants, give 29.2 to 31.6 million at 1 in 10, above the population in every variant. From 1990-91 (35 seasons) only the 1-in-10 factor is supported, at 0.762. The failure probability is a range, 2.3% to 19.4% a year (median 7.6%), and is out of the conclusions.

**3.6 "Projections are invoked but not analysed."** Change for the national comparison, scope for the rest. Capacity is held at 2025 data. Neither official baseline reaches 41.5 million within its printed years; extending their last slopes they reach it in 2066 and 2068, and both pass the 1-in-10 capacity of 30.8 million in 2034. The 2062-63 projection is 2.6 times the long-term capacity. The paper uses the baselines only; its wording on regional projections is replaced in the third round (below).

**3.7 "Per-person requirements are fixed while the population's composition changes."** Scope. Supplementary Section S10 gives the mean requirement of the 2010 population by age and sex, 8.30 MJ a day, 1.038 times the model's 8.0. Capacity is inversely proportional to the food amount, and the sampled energy range (1.000 to 1.286 times the base) contains 1.038. The change from 2011 to 2025, and regional structure, are not computed, because age-sex populations for those dates are not held. Non-resident workforces and visitors are not modelled.

**3.8 "The scale curve is mostly a consequence of the substitution rule and the partition."** Change. Section 5 is titled as the partition effect and says "The loss is a partition effect, not an effect of size." The curve is on state yields (SA2 sum 37%); the own-yield bound, 54%, is one sentence. In 200 random groupings of the regions into seven, the median is 91% of national capacity on the national yield vector, against 76% for the seven states and 33% for the 53 regions; about 180 of 200 groupings keep more than the states. Section 6 writes out the substitution rule. Letting dairy use cropland moves the SA2 share from 37% to 40%. A version in which pasture carries crops is not reported.

**3.9 "Missing literature."** Answered in the third round (below): section 1.2 now carries the paragraph.

**3.10 Presentation.** Change. Release names and file paths are out of the main text and captions; Supplementary Section S11 keys them. One ratio, capacity over population, is used throughout. Main-text figures carry two or three significant figures. Section 11 is one paragraph and one table, with detail in Supplementary Section S6. Workflow language is out of the supplement, and section 1 is numbered 1.1 to 1.4. The eight figures will be supplied as separate files, and the references and declarations will follow the journal's form.

**Minor issues.** Taken: 1 to 5, 7 to 19 (among them: Davies's span named as longer than the 14 years; overlaps in the Table 2 note; "availability, not intake"; the consumption layer is almost wholly diet; "long-term preset"; a box on what the bad-year results can bear; regions chosen by the author from a list of large movers; the form of references in 3.10), 22. Not recorded: 6. Stand: 20 (Bolleter's own citation line says 2018, 55:2; Gifford et al. is cited through Henry and Godden), 21 (the article prints "Mcdougall").

## Second referee (land systems)

I thank the referee for the report.

**1. "The two data years sit on opposite climate phases."** Rebuttal in part. Table 1 gives each window. On each three-year subset of the 2011 window, 2011 short-term capacity is 32.7 to 34.0 million and 2025 is above it by 22% to 27% (crops alone move it by under 0.1 million). The herd-cycle range for 2025, 39.4 to 43.8 million, lies wholly above 33.3. A smaller recorded grazing area lowers capacity in the land layer and raises the livestock yield by the same ratio, so the two are read as one layer, adding 2.46 million. The meat balance for 2010-12 (1.51) is close to the model's 1.49. A cereal normalisation of meat output would use the factor that point 5 asks me to treat as a bound, so I have not applied it. The paper does not label the climate phase of each window.

**2. "The land layer mixes a real change with changes in the ABS collection frame."** Change, with one request declined. The paper states the 2016 category change and the $5,000 scope level to 2008-09 (ABARE 2010), and says the threshold and frame changes after 2008-09 are not documented in the sources read here; I have not read the ABS notes and do not state a level after 2008-09. Land and livestock are one layer (see 3.2 above). Bounds of 0.4 and 2.5 in place of 0.5 and 2 leave every flip in its class (Supplementary Section S10). I have not rerun the land layer on the ABARES land-use maps; that is my choice. Livestock yields divide output by the same grazing area, so a change of area definition cancels nationally when the layers are read together, and the residue is at most 0.19 million.

**3. "Short-term capacity is close to a ratio of current livestock output to domestic consumption."** Change. Section 6 states it: "Short-term capacity therefore reads as a statement of how far meat production covers meat eaten, at the herd and diet of each year." The ratios are 1.57 against 1.50 (2021-23) and 1.51 against 1.49 (2010-12). It is a consistency check, not a validation: the matching ratio is all meat, including pork and poultry, and at the model's own meat requirement the same production feeds 33 and 28 million against 41.5 and 33.3.

**4. "Grain-fed and pasture-fed livestock are not separated cleanly."** Rebuttal, with one test not run. Section 6: grain-fed output is in the cattle-meat numerator, and the land that grows the feedlot grain and the feedlot's own land are in its denominator, so it is not credited to pasture alone. Cropland that grazes animals "is credited square metre for square metre at pasture's own output, and pasture never grows crops." Moving feed-grain land to the cropland account would leave the pooled national land need unchanged; this is argued from the land rule, not computed, and no regional test has been run. A test letting the arable share of grazing land carry crops is not reported.

**5. "The headline drought results rest on a cereal factor applied to pasture-fed output."** Change. The abstract and conclusions give both bounds, 30.8 and 41.2 million at 1 in 10; the paper says the factor is untested on pasture and about 97% of the 1-in-10 effect falls on pasture-fed food. The variants and the failure range are in 3.5 above. The single-year series is rebuilt: all 128 year cells reproduce, and where the livestock series is held the range is 43.3 to 55.8 million (43.5 to 55.4 as built); the drought years 2018-19 and 2019-20 are on placeholders for wool, feedlot grain and milk, so they say nothing about destocking; the third round corrects this reading (below). No grazing record is held, so no pasture-side factor is built, and the limitations say so. Regions take their state's factor.

**6. "The 2011 to 2025 change is not tested."** Change; see 3.4 and 3.5 above. Both years share one weather table in Table 7.

**7. "The regions are 2011 NRM boundaries."** Scope. Section 2 says the 53 regions are "the 2011 NRM regions kept as a fixed partition in both years, not as today's administrative regions." Mapping the results to current state planning regions needs new boundaries and a new allocation. SA4 results are in Table 4.

**8. "The diet layer rests on one FAO series change."** Change. The paper says FAO supply is availability, not intake, and that no second consumption series is held. On the ten-year window 2014 to 2023 short-term capacity is 39.1 million (39.7 on unrounded inputs) instead of 41.5; the window is worth about 2.4 million, about 30% of the 8.2 million rise (22% unrounded), and 2025 stays 17% to 19% above 2011.

**9. "Missing literature."** Answered in the third round (below), for Peters and colleagues (2016) in sections 1.2 and 6.

**10. "The long-term preset is mainly an energy and weather scenario."** Change. The paper calls it the long-term preset, defines it in the abstract, and names the weather and fuel settings as setting most of the figure (34.3 million without the weather allowance; 3.7 with short-term fuel). At a 1-in-20 weather factor in place of 1 in 100 it is 21.7 million, 79% of the 2025 population; this is arithmetic on checked cells, not a model run.

**Minor issues.** Taken: 1 to 14 (among them: areas in km2 with a sentence on g/m2; section 6 reconciles Lane (2014) with the cropland test; Anderson's figure is for Australia and East Africa together; "pooling gain of the closed model"; 200 groupings). Stand: 15 (as 20 above).

### First referee (population and environment)

**Literature (3.9; change 1).** Section 1.2 gains a paragraph of 150 words citing Cohen (1995), Peters and colleagues (2016), Zumkehr and Campbell (2015), Fader and colleagues (2013) and Gerten and colleagues (2020), and says where Australia's national margin sits among them. **Projections (3.6; change 2).** Section 8.1 now reads: "State government projections were not aggregated to these 53 regions, and official low and high series exist and were not used."

**Age and sex (3.7; change 3).** Section 2 gives the age-sex weighted requirement of the 2010 population, 8.30 MJ a day at reference body weights, 1.038 times the model's 8.0 MJ, so both years' capacities are about 4% high; the change in age structure from 2011 to 2025 was not computed, and non-resident populations are not counted. Section 12 says the model counts residents only. The national age-sex populations at both dates are a fetch for the author (Supplementary Section S10).

The opening of section 5 says the dependence of the loss on the pasture rule is argued, not measured, and gives the dairy test. The footnote now refers to an errata list in the archive, the bracketed flags are out of the declarations (the claims file carries them), and section 5.2 gives 28,600.

**The four wording problems.** The abstract and conclusions give one value for the ten-year diet window, 39.1 million; section 4 names 39.7 million once, as the reading on unrounded inputs, and says that the 41.5 million headline is the same on rounded and unrounded inputs (Supplementary Table S15). **Stands.** The headings of the references and declarations stay in the form of the author's file until the journal's guidelines are applied; the guidelines are a task for the author.

### Second referee (land systems)

**Climate phase (1).** Table 1 carries a note, and section 4 a sentence, saying that the 2011 windows fall in the Millennium Drought and the 2025 windows include the La Niña seasons 2020-21 to 2022-23, and that the rise includes a weather component that the paper does not separate. The ten 2011 subsets of Table S16 lie inside the drought window, so they show stability within it, not that it is typical. The paired test of section 9.1 bounds the change on shared settings; it does not remove the weather of the windows, and no other bound is held. The Table 1 note also says which field-crop window is the base (2022-23 to 2024-25) and what the 2016-17 to 2020-21 cubes supply.

**Livestock denominator (4).** Section 6, section 4, Supplementary Table S11 and Section S1 now say the same: each product's output is divided by its dry-sheep-equivalent share of the grazing area of the land layer plus the hay area, and cattle and sheep meat also divide by the land that grows the feedlot grain and the feedlot's own area. Section 12 says that no checked test has let arable grazing land in the mixed-farming zone carry crops, so the partition loss there may be overstated.

**Literature (9).** Section 6 cites Peters and colleagues (2016) on the least of three land limits and the effect of partitioning land.

**New problems.** Section 6 now says short-term capacity "is close to" a statement of how far meat production covers meat eaten. Section 8.1 reads: "over a drought and the year after it, the effect on pasture-fed output is expected to lie between them." The single-year paragraph of Supplementary Section S4 now says which years carry observed livestock output (2020-21 to 2024-25 on the rebuilt series; none as built), names 2016-17 to 2019-20 as placeholders, and withdraws the reading that the drought years show destocking. My answer to point 5 of the second round rested on that reading and is corrected here. The range 43.5 to 55.4 million stays as built; the rebuilt range, 43.3 to 55.8, is given beside it.

**Stands and open items.** The ABS explanatory notes to the agricultural census and a check of the irrigated share against ABS water use on farms remain fetches for the author, and section 12.3 still relies on the irrigated share. The parenthesis on the 52 NRM regions is out of Supplementary Section S2. The second-hand citation of Gifford and colleagues (1975) stands, as the referee allows.
