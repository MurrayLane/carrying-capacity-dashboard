## 9. How sure are the regional results

### 9.1 The ranges and the status flips

This section tests the regional results for the 53 regions, each on its own yields. The national and regional ranges come from 2,000 Latin hypercube draws of 19 sampled choices, 18 of which vary, run for every region (Supplementary Section S4 lists the choices).

On the corrected 2025 method, the 5th to 95th percentile of national capacity is 31.1 to 49.2 million people in the short term, a factor of 1.58, and 13.3 to 18.8 million in the long term. Three choices carry four-fifths of the short-term variance: the source of the land areas (32%), the energy requirement per person (27%) and the window of herd and meat data (24%). Each rests on a judgement: equal chances between two land sources and between three meat windows, and an activity level of 1.8 at the top of the energy range (the source gives 1.2 to 2.2). If the meat window of the 13-choice draws is restricted to the four windows that span a full herd cycle, the short-term range is 39.4 to 43.8 million and the long-term range 13.9 to 16.2 million, and the red-meat share of the diet then explains 85% of the short-term variance.

2011 was not sampled on the 2025-only choices. On the seven choices both years share, drawn with common random numbers, 2025 short-term capacity is above 2011's in all 2,000 paired draws: by 6.5 to 8.4 million (5th to 95th percentile) on those choices alone, and by 2.3 to 16.5 million when the 2025-only data choices are drawn for 2025 as well (1.9 to 9.6 million on holdings land only, the same land source in both years). The 13% of 2025 draws below 2011's base value is an unpaired comparison. The long-term change is not established: 26% of paired draws show a fall (52% on holdings land only), and none do on the shared choices alone, so the long-term difference is carried by the 2025-only choices and the land source. Long-term capacity changed little (paired difference -0.9 to +3.4 million) while the population rose 24%.

Each status flip was tested against its region's 2025 range on the 19 choices. A flip holds when the 2025 population lies outside the region's 5th to 95th percentile of capacity; the test covers the 2025 status only, because the 2011 inputs have no draws. Of the eight short-term flips of section 3, none is both free of an artefact flag and robust. Seven do not keep their 2025 status: Wet Tropics, Desert Channels, Mackay Whitsunday, Rangelands (WA), North Tasmania, Southern Gulf and Corangamite, the one region that moved from under to over capacity. SA Arid Lands keeps its status but is a land-definition case. The land source and the energy requirement widen the ranges: North Tasmania is over capacity in 27% of draws on holdings land and none on the raster land, Mackay Whitsunday in 16% and 43%, so the overall shares depend on the equal chances given to the two sources. Of the three long-term flips, one passes: South West Queensland, whose 2025 capacity runs from 17,200 to 24,800 (5th to 95th percentile; lowest draw 15,500) against a population of 7,110. Desert Channels and SA Arid Lands do not. The method tier is not a flip test: it moves Corangamite in the short term between the corrected and improved 2025 methods, and Mackay Whitsunday on 2011 data.

### 9.2 What each method choice does, region by region

Each method choice was applied alone to the published method on 2025 data, the published method of Lane (2014). Its effect is given as a share of the published capacity (Figure 7; Supplementary Section S4 and Table S3). Two modules are the corrections of section 2, the beef carcase yield and the biodiesel yield; the other six are improvements. The beef carcase correction lowers the short-term national capacity by 16.0% and leaves the long-term figure unchanged. The biodiesel correction is a long-term correction, +20.7% nationally and +97.9% in South East Queensland. Dairy on land that can carry it is the largest improvement, +20.2% in the short term nationally, and it moves regions both ways.

## 11. Discussion: what would widen the margin

A measure is worth what it does to the constraint that binds. Each measure in Table 9 was applied alone to the corrected 2025 data, against a reference of 41.5 million in the short term and 15.6 million in the long term, and each is free of cost in the model. Short-term capacity is bound by pasture-fed meat and dairy (section 6), so measures aimed at field crops, irrigation or cropland taken from pasture move it by only 0 to 2.4%; livestock output, diet and waste move it most. The long term is set by the weather allowance and by cropland, so drought-proofing and grain stores move it most; both rest on the cereal factor. Trade in products between regions could recover at most the pooling gain of the closed model, 15.5 million people in the short term (the national 41.5 million less the regional sum of 26.0 million), which assumes products move without loss or cost; a pool of regions does not escape a widespread drought (section 8.4). The effects are not additive, and cost, time and feasibility are not modelled. Supplementary Section S6 gives the measures with their sources, and the measures the model cannot carry.

## 12. Limitations and conclusions

The model is a closed land-use account for each region. It carries no trade, disease or biosecurity, and no limits from nutrients, soil or labour. It leaves out Indigenous food systems and wild harvest. Its named climate scenarios scale field-crop yields only; its weather allowance scales every yield by a cereal factor that is untested for pasture, and no grazing record is used. It counts residents only, not visitors or non-resident workforces. The 37% and the partition effect rest on the rule that pasture cannot carry crops, which no checked test has relaxed for arable grazing land in the mixed-farming zone, so the loss there may be overstated. 2011 was not sampled on the 2025-only choices, and five zones carry older 2011 boundaries.

### 12.1 Self-sufficiency as a policy concept

Self-sufficiency means production relative to consumption, so it does not rule out trade (Clapp 2017, p. 89). Local self-sufficiency is rarely possible: only 11 to 28% of the world's people could meet their demand for a given crop from within 100 km (Kinnunen et al. 2020, p. 229). The closed region of this paper is therefore a bound, not a policy goal.

### 12.2 Urban agriculture and other adaptations

Under forced local self-sufficiency, diet raises capacity at every scale but does not close the partition loss. On 2025 data and the state-yield basis, the sum of SA2 capacities stays between 33% and 45% of the national capacity on the same diet (Supplementary Section S7). Urban agriculture could supply 15.0% of food energy for the residents of the Sydney blocks modelled, in a 25% land-use scenario (Mcdougall et al. 2020, p. 8).

### 12.3 Water for food

The model has no water limit, so its irrigation results are an upper bound. Raising the irrigated share of all farmland from 0.7% to 5% adds 2.4% to the 2025 short term.

### 12.4 Fuel and energy in food production

Fuel enters only through the liquid fuel setting, the second-largest long-term setting (section 7). With the biofuel share at 1 and the other settings at their short-term values, the 2025 short term falls from 41.5 to 9.7 million. Australia's emergency oil stocks equalled 61 days of net imports against an obligation of 90 days (Soliman Hunter and Taylor 2021, section 5.1).

**Conclusions.** First, at fixed settings national short-term capacity rose from 33.3 to 41.5 million on the corrected method; the change is positive in every paired draw on the choices both years share, and the long-term change is not established. Averaged over all orderings of eight layers, the consumption and red-meat-mix layers added 5.6 million and land and livestock together 2.5 million; on a ten-year diet window the 2025 figure is 39.1 million. Population changes status, not capacity: capacity over population moved from 1.49 to 1.50 in the short term and from 0.69 to 0.57 in the long term. Second, the long-term preset covered 69% of the population in 2011 and 57% in 2025, and 79% in 2025 on the 1-in-20 weather factor. Third, at the base settings the worst of ten years leaves 30.8 million against 27.6 million people in 2025, and capacity is 0.95 times the population in a 1-in-20 year in both years on one weather table; at 1 in 10 and at 1 in 20 the range across the 19 choices contains the population, and on crops and grain-fed animals only the 1-in-10 figure is 41.2 million. Fourth, no short-term flip is both robust across 19 choices and free of an artefact flag; South West Queensland's long-term flip is robust. The short-term count of regions over capacity is robust to the method tier; the long-term count is not. Fifth, on the state-yield basis the sum of SA2 capacities is 37% of national short-term capacity, a partition effect of the model's substitution rule.
