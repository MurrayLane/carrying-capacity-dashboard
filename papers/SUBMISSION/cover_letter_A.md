I submit "[final title: Australia's carrying capacity 2011 to 2025 by region on the corrected releases]" as a Research Article.

The paper asks how many people Australia's regions can feed from their own land, with no trade, and how that has changed. It runs one land-use model, with its method frozen, on 2011 and 2025 data for 53 regions. At fixed settings short-term capacity rose from 33.3 to 41.5 million people while the population rose from 22.3 to 27.6 million. The paper separates the contribution of diet, land and livestock, and yields; shows which regions change between under and over capacity and how robust each change is across a wide set of model choices; reports the long-term preset and weather shocks by region; and shows how national capacity falls as the unit of self-sufficiency shrinks.

Population and Environment is the right journal because it published the two earlier papers this one extends: the scale argument (Lane, Dawes and Grace 2015) and the short and long time frames (Lane 2017). This paper brings them forward 14 years on one footing, with the corrections below.

The paper corrects errors in my earlier publications: the beef carcase-to-retail yield, the biodiesel yield, and the 2011 regional populations. They are recorded in ERRATA.md in the repository. Notes to the journals concerned, including this one, will follow after this paper is accepted.

A companion paper compares well-known carrying-capacity methods on common Australian data and tests this model among them. It is [under review at Ecological Economics / in preparation].
