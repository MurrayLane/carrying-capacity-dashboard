I submit "[final title: well-known carrying-capacity methods tested on common Australian data]" as a Methodological and Ideological Options article.

Published estimates of how many people Australia can feed differ by an order of magnitude, and they answer different questions. The paper runs five well-known methods (energy and protein balance, ecological footprint, the HALF index, people per hectare and the commodity balance) and my own land-use model on one land base and food balance (FAOSTAT, 2021 to 2023). It states the access I had and the assumptions I made for each, and reports each of the authors' figures as reproduced, reproduced in part or not reproduced on that basis. It then equalises inputs step by step to show how much of the spread is definition and how much is setting, and it gives the model's own sensitivity to its design choices.

Ecological Economics is the right home because the paper is about what carrying-capacity and footprint figures mean and how they should be reported. It ends with a twelve-item reporting checklist and a table of which method suits which purpose.

The model is my own, and I declare it as a competing interest: it was the reference of the comparison and the instrument of one section, and its ratings in the purpose table are my judgement, which the paper says.

A companion paper, the Australian regional results of the same model from 2011 to 2025, is [in preparation / under review at Population and Environment]. Both papers draw on the public repository https://github.com/MurrayLane/carrying-capacity-dashboard, archived at https://doi.org/[DOI]; every number in the paper traces to a file in it.
