# Response to the referees: "Well-known carrying-capacity methods tested on common Australian data"

## To the editor

I thank both referees for their reports. The revision makes three large changes. (1) The weather result is no longer a range: the paper gives a weather allowance and the removal of avoidable waste one at a time from 41.5 million, with a crop-only bound (40.4 million) beside every drought figure, because the drought factor is untested on pasture-fed output. (2) The shares closed by harmonising are ranges that lead with the measures needing no reference point, and a third line also equalises the per-person requirement and waste. (3) A bridge from HALF to the model replaces "consistent with" in section 5.14, Table 8 is split into three panels, and Table 9 is the author's judgement. Four requests are not taken, with reasons below: a reading from the national footprint accounts, a model run on the FAOSTAT layer, a model run with exports counted, and ratings from people outside the project.

## Referee on ecological economics

**M1. "A units mismatch, not the footprint's reading."** Changed. Section 5.11: "Line (b) is the comparison: the footprint's accounting by land type, its pooled reading being this study's sum of the land types with no equivalence factor. Line (a) is a counterfactual sensitivity, Australia's physical hectares divided by a per-person footprint in world-average hectares. Neither line is a capacity reading that HALF's authors, who call the index not predictive, make." Table 3 labels that column a counterfactual. Cropland and pasture are summed as world-average hectares, no equivalence factor (section 5.4). The abstract gives the own-productivity range (65 to 211 million) followed by the footprint's counterfactual, 146 to 343 million; the version you read gave 134 to 343 and three to eight times, which wrongly included the energy balance (your minor 1).

**M2. National accounts.** Not taken. The public data platform's country list has no Australian row, and a licence for the underlying data is outside this study (the author's choice). The Simpson food row is kept as a recipe that can be run on common data; Table 3 marks it with a dagger.

**M3. Production-to-consumption ratios.** Changed. Section 5.11: "At Australia's own productivity the footprint is the population times the ratio of Australian output to consumption in world-average hectares (build 1: 2.47 pooled), so it counts exports as the export-inclusive counts do." The binding-land-type reading, 53.9 to 77.9 million (1.30 to 1.88 times the model), sits beside the pooled 64.8 to 117.0 in 5.10 and Tables 3 and 5. The decomposition is the bridge (modeller M4).

## Referee on modelling

**M1. Capacities the author constructs.** Changed in part. Sections 4.3, 4.5, 5.5, 5.6 and 5.11 say method by method that the capacity reading is this study's. One premise is mistaken: the footprint gives no count of people in its authors' hands, and section 4.5 says it "becomes a capacity only when a country's own land is divided by the land a person needs." Only the energy and protein balance gives a count. Its closure alone is 8.2% on both lines, and 10.5% with the footprint and HALF left out. The fish reading, a single commodity with no substitution (section S3.5), stays in the base case; dropping it is a variant (5.9% and 40.9%). A column separating methods that give a count is not added.

**M2. Harmonisation.** Changed in part. Section S3.8 tabulates each method's basis as run and harmonised (energy per person 15.6, 11.3, 14.8 and 8.0 MJ; population; land; years; waste). Line (c) is the third line; it closes 14.7% of the distance to the model. The land scale-up classed as method gives 1.4% and 35.8% (energy balance alone). On population the premise is mistaken: the 27.6 million enters no method reading and no capacity in the bridge (sections 5.1, S3.8). The model is not run on the FAOSTAT layer (the author's choice): its yields are zone-level ABS series and FAOSTAT is national, so that is a different data layer. Section S3.13 sets the model's production inputs against FAOSTAT's item by item (field crops equal; fruit and vegetables a median absolute difference of 13.1%; meat inputs not comparable). The abstract and section 5.1 say "a common land base and food balance", the model keeping its own diet and production layer.

**M3. Anchoring.** Changed. See ecological economist M5. Table 6 leads with the spread of centres (0.646 to 0.702 on line (a), to 0.440 on line (b)) and adds distances to the median and geometric mean of the centres. Highlight 2: "If productivity is an input, footprint and HALF close most of their gap to the model".

**M4. Attribution.** Changed. Section 5.14 and Table 7 give the bridge from HALF at Australian productivity (76.0 million) to the model (41.5). Averaged over every ordering of seven features (5,040; forward and reverse shown), diet composition lowers the reading by 22.6 million, the pasture-fed yield layer 13.0, the cropland yield layer 6.4, the pooling rule 6.3, non-food land 4.4 and waste 17.9, and the lower per-person requirement raises it by 36.2; requirement and waste have opposite signs and cannot be separated. On overshoot, 81.7 lies inside the footprint's pooled span of 64.8 to 84.6 and above HALF's 76.0, and the third-round text below replaces "a sufficient cause" (see the closing section).

**M5. The binding parameter.** Premise mistaken in part; drought point accepted. Pasture output is national meat output shared among zones by dry sheep equivalent, so its uncertainty is the meat window and calibration, both drawn (first-order indices 0.24 and 0.03 short term); the supplement listed it as undrawn in error, and section S5 is corrected. A deviation of national pasture output from the meat series is not drawn; one at a time, 20% either way moves 41.5 to 33.4 and 49.5 million, "an illustration at 20% either way, not a bound". PAL 1.2 to 2.2 gives 48.0 and 26.9 million (1.78), against 1.27 for the drawn window. Section 5.8 gives 5.8% to 6.1% a year beside "below 1% on crops alone" and says the failure view does not cover the binding constraint. No livestock series for 1982-83 or 2002-03 is held, so none tests the allowance. The Monte Carlo is not redrawn with a pasture-output setting (the author's choice).

## Third round

Both referees recommend minor revision and ask for wording changes and one rescale. Each point is answered below with the section that carries it.

### Both referees

**The pairing is no longer called a sufficient cause.** The abstract, highlight 4 and sections 5.14, 7.6 and 9.1 say: at HALF's own land exchange rate the pooling rule accounts for 18% of the 34.5 million gap; relaxing the pairing of pasture-fed food with crops closes the gap to HALF only when pasture-fed food is replaced at equal energy (variant 1, 81.7 million, protein 93 to 81 g a day) or at equal energy and protein (85.4 million). The sentence "a sufficient cause of the gap, not shown to be the only one" is out of the paper.

**Line (c) multiples.** Section 5.10 and highlight 3 give the multiples beside "1.6 to 2.8 times the model": on one requirement and waste the line (b) readings scale by 1.51, and the footprint reads 98.0 to 176.9 million (2.4 to 4.3 times the model), HALF 114.9 (2.8) and the energy and protein balance 219.6 to 225.0 (5.3 to 5.4). Section 5.14 says that HALF at 114.9 million is above variant 1's 81.7, which carries the model's own requirement and waste. Section 5.11 says the 1.51 conversion assumes land a person scales in proportion to food energy (ecological economist M6).

### Ecological economist

**Model and failure view "partly suited" (Table 9, 7.2, 7.6, S7).** Done. The weather factor is untested on pasture-fed output, which is the binding constraint, so the model and its failure view are rated partly suited for purpose 1. The second rater rated both well suited and has not seen the change; section 7.1 and Table S15 say so. Section 7.3 says the proportional count of exports is a ratio between methods and not a model run. Section S7 describes one model column.

**Line (b) as the comparison.** Done in the abstract, 5.11, 9.1 and section 1. Line (a) is the counterfactual sensitivity, "Which line holds is a classification, not a finding" is deleted, and the pooled footprint reading is described as this study's sum of the land types, the by-land-type readings being the footprint's accounting.

**Rescale to the ten-year output mean (M8).** Not given, with the reason in section S3.14: the footprint, HALF and energy balance readings scale with output item by item, so the rescale needs each recipe rerun on item-level ten-year production, and the group medians in Table S12 are not weights. It is a compute job, not done here. Section 5.1 says the readings that scale with crop output would fall. The binding-land-type footprint (53.9 to 77.9 million) rests on livestock output, 0.4% below its ten-year mean.

**National accounts (M2).** Section 5.4 states why they were not used (the public release has no Australian row, and a licence was not sought) and that the footprint results concern the 1990s food-row recipe, not the current accounts. The edition and date of the release checked are not recorded in the project files and are left to the author.

**Literature (M9).** One sentence in section 1 ties the introduction to van den Bergh and Rietveld (2004).

**Minor points.** The abstract gives the binding-land-type reading (53.9 to 77.9) and says the top of 65 to 211 is the 1990-91 energy balance (minor 2 and the new point). Buzby 2010 is replaced by Muth et al. 2011 and Lane 2014 in Tables S10 and S14. Table 2, Figures 1 to 3, the bracketed declaration fields and the DOI (minors 7, 13, 14) are not supplied; they are not changed by this revision. Minor 16 stands: section 3.1 says the selection record holds no counts by route.
