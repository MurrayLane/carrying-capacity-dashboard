# Response to the referees: "Well-known carrying-capacity methods tested on common Australian data"

## To the editor

I thank both referees for their reports. The revision makes three large changes. (1) The weather result is no longer a range: the paper gives a weather allowance and the removal of avoidable waste one at a time from 41.5 million, with a crop-only bound (40.4 million) beside every drought figure, because the drought factor is untested on pasture-fed output. (2) The shares closed by harmonising are ranges that lead with the measures needing no reference point, and a third line also equalises the per-person requirement and waste. (3) A bridge from HALF to the model replaces "consistent with" in section 5.14, Table 8 is split into three panels, and Table 9 is the author's judgement. Four requests are not taken, with reasons below: a reading from the national footprint accounts, a model run on the FAOSTAT layer, a model run with exports counted, and ratings from people outside the project.

## Referee on ecological economics

**M1. "A units mismatch, not the footprint's reading."** Changed. Section 5.11: "Line (b) is the footprint's own accounting; line (a) is a counterfactual, Australia's physical hectares divided by a per-person footprint in world-average hectares, and neither line is a capacity reading that HALF's authors, who call the index not predictive, make." Table 3 labels that column a counterfactual. Cropland and pasture are summed as world-average hectares, no equivalence factor (section 5.4). The abstract gives the own-productivity range (65 to 211 million) and then the footprint's counterfactual, 146 to 343 million, 3.5 to 8 times the model; the version you read gave 134 to 343 and three to eight times, which wrongly included the energy balance (your minor 1).

**M2. National accounts.** Not taken. The public data platform's country list has no Australian row, and a licence for the underlying data is outside this study (the author's choice). The Simpson food row is kept as a recipe that can be run on common data; Table 3 marks it with a dagger.

**M3. Production-to-consumption ratios.** Changed. Section 5.11: "At Australia's own productivity the footprint is the population times the ratio of Australian output to consumption in world-average hectares (build 1: 2.47 pooled), so it counts exports as the export-inclusive counts do." The binding-land-type reading, 53.9 to 77.9 million (1.30 to 1.88 times the model), sits beside the pooled 64.8 to 117.0 in 5.10 and Tables 3 and 5. The decomposition is the bridge (modeller M4).

**M4. The model's roles.** Changed in part. Table 6 leads with the spread measures. Table 9 is titled the author's judgement with a blind second rating by an AI checker session; the model has one column, the failure view is in the caption and not counted as a method, and the abstract draws no finding from it. The purpose definitions in section S7 are the second rater's, not mine; their terms are the model's, as you say, and S7 says so. Outside ratings are not obtained (the author's choice): the table is not offered as a finding, and section 7.1 says "No outside person has rated the table."

**M5. Robustness.** Changed. Section 5.11 and section S3.11 vary the reference (31.1, 41.5, 49.2 and 53.6 million), leave each method out, drop the fish reading, use medians for centres, and class Henry and Godden's land scale-up as method. A method's centre is the geometric mean of its readings (19 in all). Line (a) closes -1% to 7% of the distance to the model (-1% to 8% across references): about none. Line (b) closes 28% to 46% (23% to 53%), and 5% to 36% to the median of the centres. Without the footprint and HALF it closes 10.5%, so about 87% of line (b) is their conversion to Australian productivity. With the fish reading dropped: 5.9% and 40.9%. The narrowing of the spread by about a third depends on the commodity balance: it widens by 12% without it and narrows by 23% to 48% with any other method left out.

**M6. Requirement and waste.** Changed. Section 5.11 adds line (c): every method and the model on 8.0 MJ a day as eaten with the model's loss factors (readings multiplied by 1.51). It closes 14.7% of the distance to the model (10% to 16% across the loss-factor weighting and the 8.0 or 7.376 MJ requirement) and 20% of that to the median of the centres. The spread of centres narrows by 26%, against 32% on line (b).

**M7. Section 6.** Changed, one request declined. Table 8 has three panels: model changes, ratios between methods (the export rows) and other authors' factors. Bundles are marked; diets A (1.60) and B (3.77) are ranked and diet C (7.88) is an upper bound; the ladder and the 2.22 comparison are dropped. The abstract reads: "One at a time, a weather allowance for the worst year in 100 lowers the model from 41.5 to 18.9 million (40.4 on crops only), and removing avoidable waste raises it to 55.3; across the settings varied together it moves 1.58-fold (5th to 95th percentile)." The model is not run with exports: it carries no export basket (Table 8 note; section 8.2). The footprint's export ratio, 2.47, stands beside the proportional count's 2.41.

**M8. Window and land source.** The window is answered by change. FAOSTAT's 2021 to 2023 crop output is 21% above its ten-year mean and livestock output 0.4% below. Rescaling the model's yield layers to ten years moves 41.5 to 44.1 million (+6%, crops alone 41.4); to the years outside the window, 40.7 (-1.9%). The +6% is an upper indication, because the model's meat window includes a record 2024-25 cattle year (section S3.14). The other methods' output-scaled readings were not rerun on ten years. On the land source the premise is mistaken: the totals that agree to 0.01 Mha are FAOSTAT's land use and the ABS holdings. The second source drawn is the ABARES land-use raster, which counts more cropland (+17.3% short term, +38.2% long term, one at a time), so the variance share is a difference in cropland between two sources (section S3).

**M9. Literature.** Not taken for the works named (list below); the tie to van den Bergh and Rietveld (2004) is not drawn in the introduction, for the word limit.

## Referee on modelling

**M1. Capacities the author constructs.** Changed in part. Sections 4.3, 4.5, 5.5, 5.6 and 5.11 say method by method that the capacity reading is this study's. One premise is mistaken: the footprint gives no count of people in its authors' hands, and section 4.5 says it "becomes a capacity only when a country's own land is divided by the land a person needs." Only the energy and protein balance gives a count. Its closure alone is 8.2% on both lines, and 10.5% with the footprint and HALF left out. The fish reading, a single commodity with no substitution (section S3.5), stays in the base case; dropping it is a variant (5.9% and 40.9%). A column separating methods that give a count is not added.

**M2. Harmonisation.** Changed in part. Section S3.8 tabulates each method's basis as run and harmonised (energy per person 15.6, 11.3, 14.8 and 8.0 MJ; population; land; years; waste). Line (c) is the third line; 14.7% of the 38.5% survives it. The land scale-up classed as method gives 1.4% and 35.8% (energy balance alone). On population the premise is mistaken: the 27.6 million enters no method reading and no capacity in the bridge (sections 5.1, S3.8). The model is not run on the FAOSTAT layer (the author's choice): its yields are zone-level ABS series and FAOSTAT is national, so that is a different data layer. Section S3.13 sets the model's production inputs against FAOSTAT's item by item (field crops equal; fruit and vegetables a median absolute difference of 13.1%; meat inputs not comparable). The abstract and section 5.1 say "a common land base and food balance", the model keeping its own diet and production layer.

**M3. Anchoring.** Changed. See ecological economist M5. Table 6 leads with the spread of centres (0.646 to 0.702 on line (a), to 0.440 on line (b)) and adds distances to the median and geometric mean of the centres. Highlight 2: "If productivity is an input, footprint and HALF close most of their gap to the model".

**M4. Attribution.** Changed. Section 5.14 and Table 7 give the bridge from HALF at Australian productivity (76.0 million) to the model (41.5). Averaged over every ordering of seven features (5,040; forward and reverse shown), diet composition lowers the reading by 22.6 million, the pasture-fed yield layer 13.0, the cropland yield layer 6.4, the pooling rule 6.3, non-food land 4.4 and waste 17.9, and the lower per-person requirement raises it by 36.2; requirement and waste have opposite signs and cannot be separated. The pooling rule (land pooled at world-average hectares) is 18% of the gap on HALF's own pooling and 60% of a larger gap with energy substitution. With protein held the model reads 85.4 million. On overshoot, 81.7 lies inside the footprint's pooled span of 64.8 to 84.6 and above HALF's 76.0, and the text reads "The pairing is a sufficient cause of the gap, not shown to be the only one."

**M5. The binding parameter.** Premise mistaken in part; drought point accepted. Pasture output is national meat output shared among zones by dry sheep equivalent, so its uncertainty is the meat window and calibration, both drawn (first-order indices 0.24 and 0.03 short term); the supplement listed it as undrawn in error, and section S5 is corrected. A deviation of national pasture output from the meat series is not drawn; one at a time, 20% either way moves 41.5 to 33.4 and 49.5 million, "an illustration at 20% either way, not a bound". PAL 1.2 to 2.2 gives 48.0 and 26.9 million (1.78), against 1.27 for the drawn window. On drought you are right: with the factor on crops alone the model reads 41.2, 41.0 and 40.4 million at 1 in 10, 20 and 100, against 30.8, 26.3 and 18.9, and in the short term 93% to 98% of the fall is the factor on pasture-fed output. Section 5.8 gives 5.8% to 6.1% a year beside "below 1% on crops alone" and says the failure view does not cover the binding constraint. No livestock series for 1982-83 or 2002-03 is held, so none tests the allowance. The Monte Carlo is not redrawn with a pasture-output setting (the author's choice).

**M6. Fidelity.** Changed in part. Table 3 marks every method graded partly or not reproduced with a dagger, and section 9.1 names the components: "not Simpson's other components or people per hectare". Section 5.6 says Cassidy's supplementary information was not read. A national-accounts reading is declined (ecological economist M2). Other methods appear for three choices: exports (Table 8, panel B: the footprint's 4.29, 2.06 and 2.47), land base (section 7.7: 31% in the energy balance against 1.09 in the model) and waste (section S3.10's intake variant). The choices were not rerun in a second method. Outside ratings are not obtained (ecological economist M4).

**M8. Presentation.** Changed in part. Regeneration notes and the mirror range are out of the captions and Table 4. Captions name each table's source file, as section 9.4's reproducibility claim requires (the author's choice).
